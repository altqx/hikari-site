The window is made of panels: Video, Audio, the Line editor, the Grid and others. You can move them, stack them as tabs, float them in windows of their own, or close them. A layout is one arrangement of them. On each edge of the window, outside the layout, is a [side bar](#side-bars) for the AI and tool panels.

Each tab has its own layout, so a file you translate and a file you time can be set up differently side by side. Moving panels never changes your subtitles and adds no undo step.

- **Editing**, the default: Video and Audio side by side, the Line editor under the Audio, and the Grid along the bottom.
- **Timing:** Editing without the Video.
- **Translation:** Editing without the Audio. Choosing it asks whether to turn on Translation mode for the file.
- **Typesetting:** a large Video, and the Line editor beside a short Grid below.
- **Karaoke:** the Video with the [Template editor](/en/docs/karaoke/template-editor) beside it, the Line editor as the tab behind it. The Audio panel spans the window's whole width under them, so a line's syllables get the whole waveform, and a short Grid sits at the bottom. Choosing it turns on Karaoke mode and folds the generated lines in the Grid.

Choosing a layout keeps the window's size and changes only the current tab. It never changes the [side bars](#side-bars). The [Timing postprocessor](/en/docs/basics/timing#timing-postprocessor) and Styles start in the tools side bar; if you move them out of it, Timing puts the Timing postprocessor beside the Audio, and Typesetting puts Styles beside the Video.

Choosing the Karaoke layout asks nothing and changes nothing in the file. It turns on the Audio panel's [Karaoke mode](/en/docs/karaoke/split-and-join#turning-karaoke-mode-on) and the Grid's [Fold generated lines](/en/docs/basics/grid#folding-generated-lines). A saved layout made from Karaoke does the same.

Choosing another layout afterwards, in this tab or another, turns off again only what Karaoke turned on:

- If Karaoke mode or folding was already on before, it stays on.
- If you switched either one yourself while in the Karaoke layout, it stays as you left it.

The **Line | Karaoke** switch and **Fold generated lines** work in any layout. Showing another tab, opening a file and **Reset this layout** switch neither.

### Typing applies itself

In the Karaoke layout, the Line editor applies what you type on its own, with no <kbd>Enter</kbd>:

- The text is applied 300 ms after your last keystroke, or at once when the field loses focus, so a click on the karaoke bar or the Grid always works on the text you see.
- The Grid, the karaoke bar and the video follow each time it is applied.
- One burst of typing is one undo step. A pause longer than about a second starts a new step, and so do a line change, <kbd>Enter</kbd>, Undo and saving. Until a burst is applied, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> in the field still undoes keystroke by keystroke.

It applies to the text field, and to the Translated text in translation mode. It is always on in the Karaoke layout and has no setting. Every other layout keeps a [draft](/en/docs/basics/line-editor#applying-changes) until you press <kbd>Enter</kbd> or choose another line.

The layout button in the title bar, after the menus, shows the tab's layout. Click it, or use **View › Layout**:

- The built-in layouts, then your saved layouts.
- **Save layout as…** saves the tab's arrangement under a name.
- **Reset this layout** puts the tab back the way its layout had it, undoing the moves you made since.
- **Manage layouts…** renames and deletes saved layouts.

From the keyboard, press <kbd>Alt</kbd> or <kbd>F10</kbd> to reach the menus, then the arrow keys to reach the button.

## Saving your own

Arrange the panels as you like, then choose **Save layout as…** and type a name of up to 64 characters. Saving under a name already used replaces that layout; the window warns you and its button reads **Replace**. The built-in layouts' names can't be used.

A saved layout is a snapshot: moving panels afterwards doesn't change it until you save it again. It can be used from any tab.

While a folder is open as a Project, it can have saved layouts of its own, listed above yours. See [Project tiers](/en/docs/basics/project-tiers#font-catalogues-layouts-shapes-and-tags).

## Moving panels

- **Drag a panel's header** to move it. A highlight shows where it will land: over another panel's header or middle, it becomes a tab there; near a side, it splits that panel; along the window's edge, it takes the whole edge. Let go anywhere else and it floats.
- **Double-click the header** to float the panel, or dock a floating one back.
- **Drag the line between two panels** to resize them. A double-click on the line shares the room between them equally.
- **Drag where lines meet** to resize in both directions at once. Where lines between panels meet, in a T or a cross, the pointer shows the move cursor, and a drag moves every line that meets there: the lines across follow the pointer up and down, the others left and right. Each line stops at its panels' minimum size while the others go on.

### Floating panels

A floating panel docks back the same way, on Windows and Linux alike: press anywhere on its header except the **⋯** button, such as the title or the empty part, and drag. The highlight shows where it can go, and letting go docks it there. On Wayland the floating panel steps out of the way while you drag, so you can drop it onto the panel it was covering.

On Windows and X11 the window follows the pointer while you drag; let go away from a highlight and it stays floating there. Your desktop's own way of moving windows, with its snapping or tiling, stays on its own binding, such as <kbd>Super</kbd>+drag on Hyprland. A double-click on the header docks the panel, and a right-click opens its menu.

To resize a floating panel, drag any of its four edges or four corners.

Each panel's **⋯** button, or a right-click on its header, opens its menu:

- the panel's own options, if it has any
- **Move panel…**
- **Move tab left** and **Move tab right**, for a panel among tabs
- **Float**, or **Dock** for a floating panel
- **Lock layout**: see [Locking the layout](#locking-the-layout)
- **About** and the plugin's name, for a [plugin panel](/en/docs/plugins/using-plugins#plugin-panels)
- **Close**, on its own after a line

Closing a panel only hides it. **View › Panels** lists every panel, each with **Show**, **Float** or **Dock**, and **Close**. [Plugin panels](/en/docs/plugins/using-plugins#plugin-panels) are listed there too, after HikariSub's own, and a layout keeps their places.

### Locking the layout

**View › Layout › Lock layout**, also in the layout switcher's menu and each panel's **⋯** menu, keeps every panel where it is. Nothing can be dragged, floated, docked elsewhere or moved among tabs, though you can still resize panels by their edges. The presets, saved layouts, **Reset this layout** and **View › Reset side bars** are greyed out until you unlock, and so are the panel menu's moving rows, the side bar moves among them, and **Close**.

Dock any floating panel first: the row is greyed out while one floats. The first time a drag is refused, a notice says *The layout is locked*, with **Unlock**. A command that shows a panel, such as Find, still shows it in its place, docked. The lock is kept with the tab, the session, the subtitle file's layout and saved layouts.

### Where a panel opens

A panel in a [side bar](#side-bars) opens there, and shows the side bar if it was hidden. Any other panel you open comes back where it was last in this tab's layout. A panel that has no place in the layout opens where you can find it, never in an old hidden spot or as a floating window:

- **The Log, Spellchecker, Styles, Template editor, Original text and Timing postprocessor** each have a place of their own, and open there. The Timing postprocessor's is beside the Audio.
- **Shift times, Find and Automation** open in a column to the right of the Grid. The first one makes the column, and panels opened after it join the column as tabs. The Grid keeps its left edge and gives up width first. With the Grid closed or floating, the column goes along the right edge of the window.

Shift times and Find start in the tools side bar, so they open in the column only once you move them out of it. The built-in layouts have no place for Shift times, Find and Automation, so **Reset this layout** closes them again. Layouts you saved earlier are unchanged.

### From the keyboard

**View › Move panel…** opens a window for the panel you are in. Choose where it goes (**Tab with**, **Left of**, **Above**, **Right of**, **Below**, **Float**, **Left side bar** or **Right side bar**), next to which panel, and press **Move**. **Resize** sets its width and height in pixels. The window stays open, so you can move several panels in turn.

<kbd>F6</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>F6</kbd> move to the next or previous panel, from the left side bar's panel through the middle of the window to the right side bar's.

## Side bars

Each window has a side bar on its left edge and one on its right, outside the layout:

- **The AI side bar,** on the left, holds the [Agent](/en/docs/ai/agent) and [Review](/en/docs/ai/review#the-review-panel) panels.
- **The tools side bar,** on the right, holds the [Explorer](/en/docs/basics/projects#the-explorer), Find, Shift times, the Timing postprocessor, Styles and [QC notes](/en/docs/checking/qc-notes). In a window without a Project, it starts on Find.

A side bar shows one panel at a time, under a row of the panels' icons. Hover over an icon for the panel's name, and click it to show that panel. Clicking the icon of the panel that's showing hides the side bar.

- **Show or hide a side bar** with its button in the title bar, or with **View › Toggle left side bar** and **View › Toggle right side bar**. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>B</kbd> toggles the left one; the right one has no key at first. Opening a panel that's in a hidden side bar shows the side bar too.
- **Resize a side bar** by dragging its inner edge. It starts about 320 px wide. Drag it narrower than 240 px and it hides, keeping the width it had.
- **View › Swap side bars** switches their edges, with their panels and widths. **File › Settings › Appearance › AI side bar** sets the same thing: **Left** or **Right** for the AI side bar, the other side bar holding the tools. In a right-to-left language both are mirrored, so the AI side bar starts on the right.
- **View › Reset side bars** puts both side bars back to the panels and widths they started with.

**Toggle left side bar**, **Toggle right side bar** and **Swap side bars** are in **File › Settings › Shortcuts** too. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>B</kbd> stays **Bold**.

### Moving panels into a side bar

Any panel except the Grid, Video, Audio and the Line editor can go in either side bar, the AI panels and [plugin panels](/en/docs/plugins/using-plugins#plugin-panels) included.

- A panel's **⋯** menu has **Move to the left side bar** and **Move to the right side bar**. In a side bar, it has **Move out of the side bar**, which puts the panel back in the layout, and **Move left** and **Move right**, which move its icon along the row.
- **Move panel…** has **Left side bar** and **Right side bar** among its places.
- Drag an icon along its row to reorder it, or onto the other side bar's row to move the panel there. A panel can't yet be dragged between a side bar and the middle of the window.
- **Float** takes a panel out of its side bar into a window of its own, and **Dock** puts it back in the side bar.
- **Close** takes the panel's icon off the row, so the next time you open the panel it opens in the layout.

A side bar with no panels says *Drag a panel here*. With **Turn off all AI features** ticked in **File › Settings › AI**, an AI side bar left with no panels hides, and its toggle is greyed out.

### Side bars belong to the window

Each window has its own side bars, which stay the same whichever tab is showing. Choosing a layout, a saved layout or **Reset this layout** doesn't change them either; a panel that a layout opens shows in its side bar. HikariSub saves each window's side bars as soon as you change them, in the [session](/en/docs/basics/files-and-formats#sessions), and a new window starts with the side bars you changed last.

The first time you start this version, panels you had docked in the column to the right of the Grid move into the side bars, once: the AI panels to the left and the others to the right. The rest of your saved layouts stay as they were. On that first start, both side bars show only in a window at least 2140 px wide; a narrower window starts with the tools side bar alone. On a screen too narrow for both side bars beside the middle of the window, showing one hides the other.

## Windows

You can have more than one HikariSub window, for example one on each monitor. Each window has its own tabs, menu bar, panels and layout.

### Moving a tab to another window

- **Drag a tab out** of the tab row, up, down or past either end, and let go outside every HikariSub window. A new window opens with the tab where you let go, the size of the window it came from. It opens whole on the screen where you let go, still under the pointer: if it would hang off the edge, it moves in from that edge, and gets smaller if it doesn't fit. On Wayland, an image of the tab follows the pointer, and the desktop chooses where the new window goes.
- **Drag a tab onto another window's tab row** to move it there, at the place you let go, even from a window beside it with its tab row at the same height. Let go anywhere else over a HikariSub window, and nothing moves.
- **Right-click the tab** and choose **Move tab to new window**, or **Move tab to window ›** and the window. Each window in that list is named after its current tab, or **Home** when it has none. A tab moved this way goes at the end. The list is greyed out while there is no other window.

Both commands have no shortcut at first. Give them one in **File › Settings › Shortcuts**: **Move tab to new window**, and **Move tab to window**, which opens the list under the current tab.

A tab takes everything with it: its undo history, selection, scroll, video and audio, and its panel arrangement. Nothing is saved, reloaded or reviewed. A window whose last tab moves away closes.

A tab moved to a window with a [Project](/en/docs/basics/files-and-formats#projects) uses that window's Project from then on, and a notice there says *This tab now uses the project My Show.* A window without a Project, such as a new one, shows no notice.

A window also opens for a [Project](/en/docs/basics/files-and-formats#projects) when the current window is in use, and for files you open from a terminal with `-n`. There is no command for an empty new window.

### What each window has

- **Its own:** tabs and the current tab, its [Project](/en/docs/basics/files-and-formats#projects), the menu bar, the panels and the layout, the [side bars](#side-bars), the Line editor, the status bar, and its video and audio, which go [full screen](/en/docs/basics/video#full-screen) on their own. **Styles**, the **Template editor**, **Find**, the **Spellchecker**, **Shift times**, the **Timing postprocessor** and the **Kanji timer** work on that window's current tab. Switching windows keeps where each of them was.
- **Shared by every window:** settings and shortcuts, saved layouts, recent files, Automation, the Log, the Style manager and the comparison reference.

Each window plays its current tab's video and audio, and keeps its frame while you work in another window. Only one window plays at a time: playing in one window, from the video, the audio or the Line editor, pauses the video and stops the audio in every other window, and they keep their frames. Closing a window stops its video and audio.

Menus and shortcuts act on the window you used last. Files opened from your file manager, and [Install in HikariSub](/en/docs/plugins/using-plugins#from-the-marketplace-website) links, go to it too.

A notice or question about a tab shows in that tab's window, even while you work in another one: a save that failed, for example, or a Find question. Notices about HikariSub as a whole, such as an update or a crash report, show once, in the window you used last.

### Closing windows

Close a window with its title bar ×, **File › Close window** or <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>F4</kbd>, which closes only the window it's pressed in, on every platform. HikariSub asks only about that window's unsaved changes, in a question titled *Close window*. **Cancel** keeps the window and its tabs.

Closing the last window quits HikariSub. **File › Exit** asks about the unsaved changes of every window, in one list, and then closes them all. It has no key at first: give it one in **File › Settings › Shortcuts**, where it's listed as **Exit**.

### When a monitor is unplugged

On Windows and X11, a window whose monitor is unplugged moves at once, whole, onto the screen it overlaps most, or onto the primary screen when it overlaps none, and gets smaller if it doesn't fit.

### Windows in a session

A [session](/en/docs/basics/files-and-formats#sessions) keeps every window: each comes back with its size, its tabs in order and the same tab in front, maximised or not, and the window you used last comes to the front. On Windows and X11 each also comes back where it was, whichever window you quit from; on Wayland only its size does. A window that would be less than half on the screens there are, because a monitor has gone, comes back whole on the screen it overlaps most, or on the primary screen. Loading a session first closes the windows you have open beyond the first, after asking about their unsaved changes.

## Layouts in your files

When you save an ASS file, HikariSub writes the tab's layout into it, in a `[Hikari]` section that other programs ignore. Opening the file again, here or on another computer, brings the panels back the way you left them. A change of layout alone doesn't mark the file as changed; the next save writes it.

To keep layouts out of your files, turn off **File › Settings › Script properties › Save the panel layout in subtitle files**. SRT and the other simple formats keep their layout only in the session.

A new tab starts with a copy of the current tab's layout, or with Translation for a file in Translation mode.

## If a layout can't be restored

HikariSub saves the window's layout as you work. If it can't be restored at start, for example after an update, a bar shows the Editing layout instead and keeps the old file. **View › Restore the previous layout** goes back to the layout saved before the last one.
