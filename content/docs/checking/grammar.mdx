HikariSub can check the grammar of English lines with [Harper](https://github.com/Automattic/harper). It finds mistakes such as *a apple*, *I has* or *their going*, and also wordy phrasing, wrong capitals, punctuation and more. The [Line editor](/en/docs/basics/line-editor) underlines what it finds as you type, and the **Spellchecker** panel goes through the whole file one finding at a time.

Grammar check is off at first. It checks English only: US, UK, Canadian or Australian English. Spelling is a separate check, with its own dictionaries; see [Spelling](/en/docs/checking/spelling) and [Spelling and grammar together](#spelling-and-grammar-together).

## Harper

Harper comes with HikariSub, in every package: there is nothing to install. It runs as a separate program, `harper-ls`, beside HikariSub, and only while grammar check is on. HikariSub hands it the text through a pipe on your computer. Nothing goes over the network.

**Help › Credits** lists Harper and its licence, the Apache License 2.0.

## Turning it on

Choose an English in **File › Settings › Spelling & grammar › Grammar › Grammar check**: **English (US)**, **English (UK)**, **English (Canada)** or **English (Australia)**. **Off** turns it off again and ends the `harper-ls` program. The change applies on **OK** or **Apply**.

The [Getting started window](/en/docs/get-started/getting-started-window#editing) offers the same choice on its **Editing** step, under **Spelling**. It is saved when you press **Finish**.

## In the Line editor

While grammar check is on, the Line editor's text is checked 300 ms after you stop typing. Each finding gets a blue wavy underline. In translation mode, the Translated text is checked instead of the original.

Right-click an underlined word to see what Harper found:

At the top of the menu:

- Harper's message, with the finding's rule group below it, such as *Grammar · Miscellaneous*. Text that Harper quotes in its message is shown in quotation marks.
- Harper's suggestions, in bold. Click one to put it in. A suggestion is one undo step, *Correcting grammar errors in the text field*, and the tags in the text stay where they were.
- **Ignore this rule** turns off the finding's whole rule group: see [below](#ignore-this-rule).
- **Add “…” to dictionary**, for a misspelt word. This shows only while Harper checks spelling, that is while **Check spelling** is off.

The usual items follow. Where there is no finding, the menu is the usual one.

The Grid doesn't mark grammar findings.

## Checking the whole file

Open the **Spellchecker** panel with **Subtitles › Check spelling** or **View › Panels › Spellchecker**, and choose **Grammar** at its top. **Spelling** goes back to the spelling check. While grammar check is off, the panel says so and offers **Grammar settings…**, which opens the Settings page.

The check reads the whole file in the background, starting at the active line and going round to the line before it. While it reads, the panel shows **Checking line … of …** with a progress bar and **Cancel**. The first finding shows as soon as it is found, and you can work on it while the rest are read.

For each finding, its line is selected in the Grid and the finding in the Line editor, and the panel shows:

- Harper's message
- the line's number and the rule group
- the text around the finding, without tags, with the finding underlined. At most 40 characters show on each side, cut at a whole word, with … where text is left out
- **Replace with**, filled with the first suggestion, and the list of suggestions

| Button | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Replace** | Puts **Replace with** in place of the finding, as one undo step |
| **Ignore** | Skips this finding |
| **Ignore this rule** | Turns off the finding's rule group, and skips every finding of that group |
| **Add to dictionary** | Adds the word to your dictionary, and skips the other findings of that word. Only for a misspelt word |

Each button moves on to the next finding. When none are left, the panel says *No more grammar findings in* and the file's name, with the number of lines checked. **Check again** starts over from the active line.

Comment lines are never checked, nor are template and code lines.

## Ignore this rule

Harper has 936 rules, sorted in 15 groups. **Ignore this rule**, in the Line editor's menu or the panel, turns off the whole group the finding belongs to, not the one rule. It is saved at once.

- **Undo** turns the group back on.
- **Grammar settings…** opens **File › Settings › Spelling & grammar**, where every group has its own switch.

Like any information notice, it fades after 5 seconds. The [Log](/en/docs/help/troubleshooting#the-log-panel) keeps its text, without the buttons.

## Settings

| Setting | Does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Grammar check** | Off, or the English to check | Off |
| **Rule groups** | A switch for each of Harper's 15 groups, with its number of rules | All on |

The groups are:

| Group | Checks |
| --- | --- |
| **Proper nouns** | Names of places, organizations and products keep their standard capitalization |
| **Initialisms** | Abbreviations and initialisms use the expected letter case and punctuation |
| **Closed compounds** | Compound words written as one word instead of split apart |
| **Phrase corrections** | Common phrase-level mistakes where a standard expression is preferred |
| **Capitalization and case** | Personal pronouns, titles, brands and other case-sensitive text |
| **Punctuation and spacing** | Punctuation marks, apostrophes, quotation marks, commas and spacing |
| **Numbers and units** | Numbers, dates, times, decades and units of measurement |
| **Pronouns and possession** | Pronouns, contractions, possessives and their agreement |
| **Compounds and hyphenation** | Multi-word compounds that should be open, closed or hyphenated |
| **Spelling and orthography** | Misspellings and word forms spelled the wrong way |
| **Style and redundancy** | Wordy, repeated or weak phrasing that can be tightened |
| **Grammar and agreement** | Agreement, articles, prepositions, verb forms and sentence structure |
| **Word choice and usage** | More standard or idiomatic wording for understandable phrases |
| **Regionalisms and dialect** | Wording that may not fit the chosen English |
| **Miscellaneous** | Rules that fit none of the groups above |

A group's switch turns on only the rules Harper itself has on; a few are off in Harper by default. While **Grammar check** is Off, the switches are greyed out and keep their state.

One rule is always off: Harper's check that a sentence starts with a capital. A subtitle line is often the middle or end of a sentence, so it would mark most lines that continue one. No setting turns it on: the **Capitalization and case** switch turns only that group's other rules on and off.

## Spelling and grammar together

- While **Check spelling** is on, Harper leaves spelling to the dictionary, so a word is never marked twice. Misspellings keep their coloured background; grammar findings get the blue underline.
- While **Check spelling** is off, Harper checks spelling too, in its **Spelling and orthography** group. Its misspellings are underlined like the other findings, and their menu has **Add “…” to dictionary**.
- **Add to dictionary** adds the word to your [personal dictionary](/en/docs/checking/spelling#your-personal-dictionary), and Harper uses it too. Words you add or remove there reach Harper at once.
- Where a grammar finding and a misspelling overlap, the right-click menu shows the grammar finding.

## What is checked

Harper gets only the words of a line:

- Override tags in `{…}`, karaoke tags included, are left out, so `ha{\k20}ru` reads as *haru*.
- `\N`, `\n` and `\h` read as spaces, so a sentence broken over two lines is read as one.
- Drawings are left out.
- In SRT files, `<…>` tags are left out. In TMPlayer, MicroDVD and MPL2 files, `{…}` is left out and `|` reads as a space.

Since the tags are left out, a suggestion goes in around them: the tags stay in their order.

A line longer than 4096 characters isn't checked.

## Speed

- Nothing runs while grammar check is off.
- `harper-ls` starts at the first check after you turn grammar check on. The first findings take a moment, about half a second, while Harper loads its dictionary.
- Any change to the grammar settings, and turning **Check spelling** on or off, restarts `harper-ls`, so the next findings take that moment again.
- While it runs, `harper-ls` uses memory of its own, about 150 MB.
- The panel's check reads the file in small steps, so the window keeps answering, and **Cancel** stops it at once.

## When something goes wrong

| Problem | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| No underlines | Check that **Grammar check** isn't Off, and that the line isn't a comment. In translation mode, only the Translated text is checked |
| Too many findings of one kind | Right-click one and choose **Ignore this rule**, or turn its group off in **File › Settings › Spelling & grammar › Rule groups** |
| Harper marks names or made-up words | Turn **Check spelling** on, so your dictionary checks spelling instead, or add the words to your dictionary |
| A **Grammar check** notice says "The grammar checker (harper-ls) stopped" | `harper-ls` ended or couldn't start. The next check starts it again, and the notice doesn't repeat until a check has worked. If it keeps stopping, [report the problem](/en/docs/help/reporting-problems#report-an-issue) with the message |
| The panel says "The grammar checker stopped." | The check of the file stopped with it. Press **Check again** |
| The panel says "Another file is open in the editor." | The check stops when you switch to another file's tab. Go back and press **Check again** |
| A replace says "The line changed since it was checked." | The line was edited after it was read. Press **Check again** |
| A replace says "The text there cannot be replaced." | The finding touches a drawing or a broken tag, or the replacement contains tag characters. Edit the line by hand |

<kbd>F1</kbd> in the Spellchecker panel opens this page while the panel is in Grammar mode, and [Spelling](/en/docs/checking/spelling) while it is in Spelling mode.
