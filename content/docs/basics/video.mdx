The Video panel shows the frame your subtitles are timed against, with the subtitles drawn on it as they will look. Each tab keeps its own video, audio, keyframes and position.

## Opening a video

Use **File › Open video…** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>O</kbd>), **Video › Recently opened videos**, or drop a video file on the window. The picker lists MKV, MP4, AVI, MOV, WebM, TS, M2TS and WMV files; choose **All files** to open another kind.

Every video is indexed first, so that each frame has an exact time, variable frame rates included. While it indexes, a strip over the picture shows the progress and **Cancel**; the rest of the window stays usable.

HikariSub may ask first:

- **Subtitles with the same name** beside the video: **Open both** or **Video only**.
- **A different resolution** from the subtitles: **Resample** scales the subtitles to the video, **Change resolution only** sets the new size without moving anything, and **Keep as is** leaves them. **Resample and stretch** appears when the aspect ratios differ. You can resample later with **Subtitles › Resample subtitles**.
- **No YCbCr matrix** in the subtitles, or **a different one** from the video's: whether to set it to the video's. Each answer is one undo step.
- **No layout resolution** in the subtitles, or **a different one** from the video's: whether to set it.

It also warns when the video has no colour matrix tag, or has HDR or wide-gamut colours. **File › Settings › Video › Script resolution** has a row for each question, from **Ask** to always or never changing the file, and a check box for each warning. **Don't ask again** in a question sets its row to the answer you gave. Reopening or reloading a video asks again.

When you open subtitles that name a video, audio, keyframes or timecodes file, a bar over the picture offers to load them: **Load associated**, or **No**. Files that can't be found, a timecodes file among them, are listed under *Linked files not found:*, and when none of them can load, the bar has only **OK**.

**Video › Unload video** empties the panel. The file itself is not touched. **Video › Reload video** opens the same file again, at the frame you were on.

**Video › Video details…** lists the open video's file, resolution and aspect ratio, its exact frame rate, such as *23.976 fps (24000/1001)*, or *Variable*, its frame count and length, its colour space and its colour matrix. The matrix says when it is guessed because the file isn't tagged, and shows the subtitles' own YCbCr matrix when that differs. With a [timecodes file](#timecodes) loaded, the frame rate is labelled **Video stream frame rate**, and **Frame times from** names the timecodes file, which the frame times and the length come from.

### Formats

| Kind | What HikariSub reads |
| --- | --- |
| **Containers** | Matroska (`.mkv`, `.mka`), WebM (`.webm`), MP4 and QuickTime (`.mp4`, `.m4a`, `.mov`), AVI, MPEG-TS (`.ts`, `.m2ts`), Ogg (`.ogg`, `.opus`, `.ogm`) and ASF/WMV |
| **Video** | H.264, HEVC, MPEG-1, MPEG-2, MPEG-4, VP8, VP9 and AV1 |
| **Audio** | Vorbis, Opus, AAC, AC-3, MP3, FLAC and PCM |

**Next file** and **Previous file**, and the lookup for a video with the same name as your subtitles, count `.webm` files as videos.

**AV1** plays only with a hardware AV1 decoder: VAAPI or NVDEC on Linux, and D3D11VA, D3D12VA or DXVA2 on Windows. Without one, **Play** stops and a notice says *AV1 video can't be played on this computer. Its frames still show while paused, and seeking and stepping work.* Everything else works for AV1 on every computer: opening, the paused frames, seeking, stepping frame by frame, keyframes and the audio's waveform. VP8 and VP9 play everywhere.

A VP9 video with transparency shows its colours, and the transparency is ignored.

## The panel

Below the picture are the visual tools' bar, the seek bar, the times field and the transport buttons: **Previous file**, **Play**, **Play line**, **Stop**, **Next file**, the frame buttons and the volume.

The times field shows the frame's time and number. With an active line, it adds how many frames and milliseconds the frame is from the line's start and end. It turns the warning colour on a keyframe.

The panel's **⋯** menu sets how the video follows the Grid: **Move video to the selected line** (for example *Every line change*) and **After moving, play** (for example *Video and audio to the line end time*). **File › Settings › Video › Playback** has the same two rows.

The tools on the left of the picture are the [visual tools](/en/docs/typesetting/visual-tools).

## Playing and moving

| Key | Does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Space</kbd> | Play or pause. <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Space</kbd> works from anywhere |
| <kbd>Left</kbd> <kbd>Right</kbd> | Previous or next frame, while paused |
| <kbd>;</kbd> <kbd>L</kbd> | 5 seconds back or forward |
| <kbd>Down</kbd> <kbd>Up</kbd> | 1 minute back or forward |
| <kbd>N</kbd> <kbd>M</kbd> | Previous or next chapter |
| <kbd>,</kbd> <kbd>.</kbd> | Previous or next video in the folder, after asking |

The keys without <kbd>Alt</kbd> work while the Video panel has the keyboard. The [Shortcuts reference](/en/docs/reference/hotkeys#video) lists them all, including the commands without a default key, such as **Go to next keyframe**.

**Play line** plays the active line from its start to its end. The seek bar can be clicked or dragged, and the wheel below the picture sets the volume. **Video › Streams** chooses the audio track, and **Video › Chapters** jumps to a chapter.

## Your lines and the video

- **Video › Insert start time from video** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Left</kbd>) and **Insert end time from video** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Right</kbd>) set the selected lines' times to the current frame, as one undo step. A line next to them that is joined to one of them stays joined.
- **Video › Shift by frames to video** moves the selected lines, or the active line and every line that starts at or after it, by whole frames, so that the active line starts or ends on the current frame. See [From the video](/en/docs/basics/timing#from-the-video).
- **Video › Centre times within frames** moves the selected lines' times to the middle of their frames, so a later shift by time keeps them on whole frames. See [Centring times within frames](/en/docs/basics/timing#centring-times-within-frames).
- **Video › Align to video…** times a sign to the frames it shows on: see [Aligning to the video](#aligning-to-the-video).
- **Video › Track colour…** and **Find fade…** write a point's colour on each frame, or a `\fad`, from the video: see [Tracking a colour](/en/docs/typesetting/motion-tracking#tracking-a-colour) and [Finding a fade](/en/docs/typesetting/motion-tracking#finding-a-fade).
- **Video › Go to start time of line** and **Go to end time of line** move the video to the active line.
- <kbd>F2</kbd> selects the line at the video's time.

[Timing](/en/docs/basics/timing) covers snapping times to keyframes.

### Aligning to the video

**Video › Align to video…** times the active line to the frames where a point of the picture keeps its colour, such as a sign on a wall. It needs an open video and an active line, and has no key at first. Opening it pauses the video.

1. Click a point on the sign that keeps its colour while the line should show. The window shows its **Point X** and **Point Y** and its **Colour**. The arrow keys move the point by a pixel, or 10 with <kbd>Shift</kbd>.
2. **Tolerance** sets how far the colour may drift and still count as the same, from 0 to 255. It starts at 20 and is remembered.
3. Press **Align**, <kbd>Enter</kbd>, or double-click the point. While it searches, the window counts the frames checked, and **Cancel** stops it.

The line then starts on the first frame around the current one where the point holds, and ends at the end of the last one. It is one undo step, *Aligning to video*, and the video stays on the frame you picked. Choose a point that differs clearly from what is behind it. If a frame can't be read, nothing changes, and read-only subtitles can't be aligned.

## Keyframes

The video's own keyframes come from indexing. **Video › Open keyframes** loads a keyframes file instead: Aegisub keyframes, XviD or x264 two-pass stats, or DivX. It needs a video open. Keyframes show in the times field and as lines on the audio waveform.

## Timecodes

Frame times come from the indexed video. **Video › Open timecodes** loads a timecodes file instead: v1, v2 or v4, including mkvmerge's `# timestamp format` files. The Grid's frames, the timing commands, seeking, Shift times by frames, the audio's video marker, keyframes and scripts then use its times. The video still shows its own frames, and the subtitles are drawn at the file's time.

- **Video › Recently opened timecodes** lists the files you opened.
- **Video › Save timecodes** writes the times as a v2 file.
- **Video › Close timecodes** goes back to the video's own times.

None of them has a key at first. Each tab keeps its own timecodes file. Opening another video drops it, and unloading the video keeps it, so Shift times by frames works from the file alone. A file listing fewer frames than the video gives one warning. The status bar's frame rate then shows the file's average rate, and its tip names the file.

Saving an ASS file keeps the timecodes file's path in it, as Aegisub does. Reopening the file offers to load it with the video, and the last session brings it back.

## Zoom and pan

The video zooms and pans as in Aegisub 3.5:

- <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+wheel zooms around the pointer, from 12.5% to 1000%.
- <kbd>Shift</kbd>+wheel pans.
- Dragging with the middle button pans. The drag starts once the pointer has moved 3 pixels, so a middle click that doesn't move still reaches the visual tool.
- A touchpad pinch zooms.

The plain wheel does nothing on the video. In Aegisub 3.5 it resizes the video box, but HikariSub's video fills a docked panel whose size the layout sets. A [visual tool](/en/docs/typesetting/visual-tools) that uses the wheel or the middle button keeps it: Hydra keeps the wheel and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+wheel, Vector clipping and Vector drawing keep the wheel, and Text X / Y rotation and Text scaling keep the middle button.

**Video › Zoom video** zooms in to the **Start zoom** (200% unless you set one), or shows the whole frame again when the video is zoomed or panned. **Video › Reset zoom and pan**, or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> over the video, shows the whole frame again, unpanned. It can also take a shortcut in **File › Settings › Shortcuts**.

### Choosing how zoom works

**File › Settings › Video › View › Zoom and pan** chooses between:

- **As in Aegisub 3.5**, the default, described above.
- **As in HikariSub legacy**, the crop zoom of earlier versions. With the Position pointer on, the plain wheel zooms around the pointer, up to 1000%. **Video › Zoom video** shows a zoom frame you can drag and resize, and <kbd>Return</kbd> ends it. Middle-drag and pinch don't pan or zoom.

With **As in Aegisub 3.5**, three rows below choose what each wheel does: **Wheel on the video**, **Ctrl+wheel on the video** and **Shift+wheel on the video**. Each one **Zooms the video**, **Zooms the video (reversed)**, **Pans the video**, **Pans the video (X and Y swapped)** or **Does nothing**. They start at **Does nothing**, **Zooms the video** and **Pans the video**. Switching between the two models shows the whole frame again.

## Aspect ratio

**Change aspect ratio** in the right-click menu stretches the picture between **Taller** and **Wider**. **Reset** gives back the video's own ratio, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> the ratio you started with. The change lasts until the next video opens.

## The right-click menu

With the Position pointer on, right-click the picture, or press <kbd>Menu</kbd> over it, for:

- **Copy video position**, **Play** or **Pause**, **Stop** and **Full screen**. With more than one monitor, **Full screen on ›** lists the others.
- **Export frame ›**: see [Saving a frame](#saving-a-frame).
- **Audio track ›** and **Chapters ›**, when the video has them.
- **Render cost**, as in the Video menu.
- **Change aspect ratio** and **Reset zoom and pan**.
- **Unload video**.

In full screen the menu also has **Open editor**, **Open video…**, **Open subtitles…**, **Recently opened ›** with your subtitles and videos, **Progress bar** and **Full screen bar ›**.

## Full screen

Double-click the picture, or press <kbd>F</kbd> over it. <kbd>Esc</kbd>, <kbd>F</kbd> or a double-click leave full screen, and leaving pauses the video. With [more than one window](/en/docs/customize/layouts#what-each-window-has), each window's video goes full screen on its own.

The bar along the bottom has the transport buttons, the times, the current line, **Open editor**, the visual tools, mute and volume. Its mode button, or <kbd>Tab</kbd>, switches between:

- **Show when the pointer moves:** the bar shows when you move the pointer, and hides after a moment of playback.
- **Always show:** the bar stays, and the picture ends above it.
- **Never show:** the picture only.

The right-click menu's **Full screen bar ›** sets the same mode.

**Open editor** leaves full screen, selects the line at the video's time and shows the Line editor. With more than one monitor, **Full screen on ›** in the right-click menu opens full screen on **Monitor 2** and so on. **Progress bar** shows or hides the progress bar in the corner.

<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Tab</kbd> moves to the bar's speaker button, and the bar stays up while it has the focus. <kbd>Menu</kbd> then opens its **Output device** menu.

## Dummy video

**Video › Open dummy video** makes a blank video to time against without the real one: choose a resolution, a colour or a checkerboard, a frame rate from 15 to 120 and a duration. The frame rate can be a fraction, such as `24000/1001`, which keeps the timing exact over long durations, and the list has 24000/1001, 30000/1001 and 60000/1001. A side can be up to 100000 pixels; over 8192 the picture is drawn smaller, but the video keeps the size you asked for. **1080x1920 (HD 1080p vertical)** is among the sizes. It brings its own silent audio as long as itself, so the audio box works too.

## Subtitles on the video

HikariSub draws subtitles with libass. On Windows, VSFilterMod can be chosen under **File › Settings › Video › Subtitles on video › Subtitle display filter**, for scripts that use its tags.

**Preview subtitles at** chooses how they are drawn:

- **Video resolution (as encoded)**, the default: at the video's size, then scaled with the picture, as most players do.
- **Screen size (sharp):** at the size the picture takes on your screen, so they stay sharp on large and high-DPI screens.

The layout is the same either way. The setting also applies to full screen and to the previews in the Style manager, the font picker and the [Template editor](/en/docs/karaoke/template-editor). It is not available with VSFilterMod.

## Saving a frame

While paused, the right-click menu's **Export frame ›** has **Save as PNG…** and **Copy**, **Save with subtitles as PNG…** and **Copy with subtitles**, and **Save subtitles only as PNG…** and **Copy subtitles only**. The last two give the subtitles alone, on a transparent background. Saved frames go beside the video, named after it and the frame's time, and never replace an earlier file. They are always at the video's size.

## Settings

**File › Settings › Video** holds the rest: whether a click pauses, the seeking method, GPU colour conversion, the preferred audio language, the start zoom and the resolution warnings. The [Settings reference](/en/docs/reference/settings#video) lists them with their defaults.
