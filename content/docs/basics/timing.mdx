Each line has a start and an end time. You can set them from the audio, from the video, or by typing them, and then shift or tidy many lines at once with the **Shift times** and **Timing postprocessor** panels.

## From the audio

The [Audio panel](/en/docs/basics/audio) is the usual way to time dialogue:

1. Select a line. Its times show as the bright range on the waveform.
2. Click to set the start, right-click to set the end, or drag the boundaries. Hold <kbd>Alt</kbd> as you let go to move the neighbouring line's boundary to the same place.
3. Play it back: <kbd>R</kbd> plays the line, <kbd>Q</kbd> the 500 ms before it and <kbd>W</kbd> the 500 ms after it.
4. With **Apply changes automatically** on, as it is at first, the times are already in the line. Otherwise press <kbd>G</kbd> or <kbd>Enter</kbd> to apply them.
5. <kbd>X</kbd> moves to the next line and plays it. **Apply changes** moves there too.

**In** (<kbd>C</kbd>) and **Out** (<kbd>V</kbd>) add a lead-in of 200 ms before the start and a lead-out of 300 ms after the end, so a line shows a moment before the voice and stays a moment after it. **File › Settings › Audio › Lead-in and lead-out** changes the amounts.

**Audio › Join with previous line using the audio selection** gives the active line the times of the audio selection, and makes the line before it in the file end where the selection starts. Both change as one undo step. It needs audio, and has no key at first.

## From the video

**Video › Insert start time from video** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Left</kbd>) sets the selected lines' start to the frame on screen, and **Insert end time from video** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Right</kbd>) their end to that frame's end. A start after the end moves the end along, and the other way round. Step through the frames with <kbd>Left</kbd> and <kbd>Right</kbd> to find the frame where a sign appears or a shot changes.

A neighbour that is joined to a selected line stays joined, even when it isn't selected. With **Insert start time from video**, the line before in the file, if it ended exactly where the selected line started, now ends at the new start. With **Insert end time from video**, the line after, if it started exactly where the selected line ended, now starts at the new end.

**Video › Shift by frames to video** moves lines by whole frames, so that the active line lines up with the frame on screen:

| Command | Moves | So that the active line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Selected lines, start at video** | The selected lines | Starts on the frame on screen |
| **Selected lines, end at video** | The selected lines | Has its last frame on the frame on screen |
| **Following lines, start at video** | The active line and every line that starts at or after it | Starts on the frame on screen |
| **Following lines, end at video** | The active line and every line that starts at or after it | Has its last frame on the frame on screen |

Every start and end moves by the same number of frames, so each line keeps its length in frames. Each shift is one undo step. The commands need a video, and have no keys at first.

**Start frame offset** and **End frame offset**, under **File › Settings › Grid › New lines**, add a fixed amount to these times.

### Centring times within frames

ASS stores times in hundredths of a second, so a time rarely falls exactly where a frame starts. A time close to the edge of its frame can cross into the next frame when you later [shift lines by a time](#shift-mode), while a time near the middle can't.

**Video › Centre times within frames** moves the selected lines' starts and ends to the hundredth nearest the middle of their frames. Each one stays on the frame it was on, so nothing changes on the video. A later shift by time then moves every line by the same number of frames.

- It acts on every selected line, hidden ones included.
- It is one undo step, and does nothing when the times are already centred.
- It needs a video, a [dummy video](/en/docs/basics/video#dummy-video) included, and has no key at first. Give it one in **File › Settings › Shortcuts**, where it is named **Centre times within frames**.

## In the Line editor

The Line editor's **Start**, **End** and **Duration** fields take typed times, h:mm:ss.cc. A typed digit replaces the next one and skips the colons, and a pasted time must be whole. Over a field, the wheel steps the part under the pointer, and dragging with the right button held steps it faster.

<kbd>Enter</kbd> applies the times and moves to the next line; <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd> applies them and stays. **Stay on the line when editing times**, under **File › Settings › Line editor**, makes <kbd>Enter</kbd> stay in a time field too. Start and End turn the warning colour while the start is after the end.

**Show video frames instead of times**, in the same place, shows frame numbers in the fields and the Grid.

## Snapping to keyframes

A keyframe usually starts a new shot. Lines that start or end on one look tidier than lines that cross a cut by a frame or two.

- <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Left</kbd> moves the active line's start to the nearest keyframe or line boundary, up to 5 seconds away, and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Right</kbd> its end. They need a video and the audio open.
- In the Audio panel, **Snap to keyframes** and **Snap to other lines** under **File › Settings › Audio › Snapping** make a dragged boundary jump to a keyframe or line near it. Hold <kbd>Shift</kbd> while dragging to snap only this once, or to not snap when they are on.
- **Video › Go to previous keyframe** and **Go to next keyframe** move the video there. They have no key at first; give them one in Settings › Shortcuts.

Keyframes come from the video. **Video › Open keyframes** loads them from a file instead, as the [Video guide](/en/docs/basics/video#keyframes) explains.

## Timing helpers

**Subtitles › Timing helpers** holds small timing commands, each one undo step. They have no key at first: <kbd>Shift</kbd>+click one in the menu, or give it one in **File › Settings › Shortcuts**.

- **Snap start to the previous keyframe**, **Snap start to the next keyframe**, **Snap end to the previous keyframe** and **Snap end to the next keyframe** move each selected line's start or end to the keyframe before or after it. A time already on a keyframe moves on to the next one. A start never passes its line's end, nor an end its start, and a line with no keyframe that way keeps its times.
- **Add lead-in to the selected lines**, **Remove lead-in from the selected lines**, **Add lead-out to the selected lines** and **Remove lead-out from the selected lines** use the lead-in and lead-out amounts in **File › Settings › Audio**. Adding stops at the line before's end, or the line after's start, so lines don't overlap; comment lines don't count. Removing always leaves the line at least one frame.
- **Move start one frame earlier**, **Move start one frame later**, **Move end one frame earlier** and **Move end one frame later** move the selected lines' start or end by one frame.
- **Extend previous line to this start** makes the line before the active one end where it starts, and **Extend next line to this end** makes the line after it start where it ends. Comment lines are skipped, and at the first or last line nothing changes.

All but the two Extend commands need a video. To shift whole lines, use [Shift times](#shift-times).

## Shift times

**Subtitles › Shift times…** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>I</kbd>) shows the **Shift times** panel (see [Where a panel opens](/en/docs/customize/layouts#where-a-panel-opens)). It moves many lines at once, for example to fit subtitles to a version of the video that starts two seconds later.

- **Shift by:** **Amount**, **To video** or **To audio mark**. **To video** needs a video and **To audio mark** an audio mark, the one you set on the audio ruler. With either, **Move** chooses whether the active line's **Start** or **End** goes there, the panel shows the time, such as *To the video at 0:12:03.45*, and every other line moves by the same amount. The amount field is then called **Plus**, and is added on top.
- **Amount:** a **Time**, or a number of **Frames**, which needs a video.
- **Direction:** **Later** or **Earlier**.
- **Lines:** **All lines**, **Selected lines**, **Selected line and below**, **Starting at or after the selection**, **Starting at or before the selection**, or **Lines in chosen styles…**, which opens a list of the styles to tick. **Choose styles…** changes them later.
- **Times:** **Both**, **Start** or **End**.
- **End times:** **Leave as they are**, **Fix overlaps** or **Recalculate from text length**. **Fix overlaps** ends each line where the next one starts, if it would overlap it. **Recalculate from text length** also gives each line that doesn't overlap the next a new end: **Time per letter** in **File › Settings › Conversion**, 110 ms at first, for each character of its text, tags included, and at least one second. Both need a video.
- **Move tag times too** (`\move`, `\t`, `\fad`) keeps those tags in step with the video when the shift lands between frames.
- **Centre times within frames** moves each shifted time, after the shift and the end-time fix, to the middle of its frame, as [Video › Centre times within frames](#centring-times-within-frames) does, so a later shift by time keeps lines on whole frames. With **Times** at **Start** or **End**, only those are centred. With **Move tag times too**, the tag times stay on their frames. It needs a video.

Times never go below zero. Hidden lines, filtered out of the Grid, are left alone.

## Timing postprocessor

**Subtitles › Timing postprocessor…** shows the **Timing postprocessor** panel. It tidies the lines' times rather than moving them, and needs a video. It has no key at first. Its **Lines** choice is its own, with the same six options as Shift times, and starts from Shift times' choice the first time.

Each rule has a switch:

- **Lead-in:** **Start earlier by** a number of milliseconds.
- **Lead-out:** **End later by** a number of milliseconds.
- **Close small gaps:** **Start back to the previous end if within** and **End on to the next start if within** a number of milliseconds. A gap no larger than the two together closes: the earlier line's end and the later line's start meet in it, in the proportion of the two limits.
- **Snap to keyframes:** **Start within** and **End within** a number of milliseconds **before** and **after** a keyframe. It needs keyframes, and leaves a line alone if snapping would make it 600 ms long or less.

They run in that order.

### The assistant

The panel's **Assistant** tab, beside **Preview**, times the lines one at a time. **Subtitles › Timing helpers › Timing assistant** opens it. Like the rules, it needs a video. For the active line, it shows the start and end now and the ones it suggests, from the rules above, with the keyframes near it. **Why** says what moved each edge, such as *Snapped to the keyframe 2 frames earlier.* or *Lead-in of 200 ms, linked to the previous line.* A rule that is off isn't used.

- **Accept** (<kbd>Enter</kbd>) sets the suggested times and goes to the next line. **Accept assistant suggestion**, in the same menu, does the same from anywhere.
- **Change** (<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>C</kbd>) lets you type other times first.
- **Skip** (<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>S</kbd>) leaves the line as it is and goes to the next.

Each accepted line is one undo step. The assistant goes through every line from the active one on, *Line 4 of 120*, skipping comment lines. With **Lines in chosen styles…** it takes only those styles; it doesn't follow the panel's other **Lines** choices. At the end it says how many lines you accepted and skipped.

## Preview and Apply

Both panels show what a run would do, beside the choices when the panel is wide and under them when it is narrow, and follow every change you make:

- A line such as *Changes 214 lines*, or for the postprocessor *Changes 31 of 120 lines*, with how many got a lead-in, a lead-out, a closed gap or a snap. *No line changes* when nothing would move.
- Up to eight lines: the selected ones, or the first lines that change when none is selected, each with its old and new start and end, and its text or the rules that moved it.

**Apply** names what it does, such as **Shift 214 lines** or **Apply to 31 lines**, and is one undo step, *Shift times* or *Timing postprocessor*. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd> applies from any field, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> goes back to the Grid.

A choice that can't run says why, and offers what it needs: *Frames need a video.*, *Needs a video.* (for the end-time fixes and for Centre times within frames) or *The rules need a video.* with **Open video…**, and *Needs keyframes.* with **Open keyframes…**. With both gap limits at 0 ms, Close small gaps says *Both gap limits are 0 ms. Raise one to close gaps.*

**Shift times with the last values** and **Timing postprocessor with the last values** run a panel's last choices without opening it. They have no key at first; give them one in **File › Settings › Shortcuts**.

## Presets

Each panel has its own presets. **Preset** shows the one in use, or *last used* once you change a value. The **⋯** menu beside it has **Save as preset…**, **Rename preset…** and **Delete preset…**. Saving under a name that exists replaces that preset, after saying so.

The Timing postprocessor's **⋯** menu also has **Use in the Timing layout**. The preset you mark shows *(Timing default)*, and is applied each time you choose the [Timing layout](/en/docs/customize/layouts#the-built-in-layouts).

Your Shift times profiles from before became presets, with the same names.

While a folder is open as a Project, it can have presets of its own, listed under *This project* above yours. See [Project tiers](/en/docs/basics/project-tiers#presets).

## Lines next to each other

**Make times continuous with the previous line** and **with the next line**, in the Grid's right-click menu, make each selected line start where the line before it ends, or end where the next one starts.

## Keys

| Key | Does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>I</kbd> | Shift times panel |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd> | Apply, in the Shift times and Timing postprocessor panels |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Left</kbd> <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Right</kbd> | Start or end time from the video |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Left</kbd> <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Right</kbd> | Start or end to the nearest keyframe |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Down</kbd> | Previous or next line |
| <kbd>C</kbd> <kbd>V</kbd> | Lead-in or lead-out, in the Audio panel |
| <kbd>G</kbd> or <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Apply changes, in the Audio panel |

Inside the Line editor, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>I</kbd> is italic instead. The [Shortcuts reference](/en/docs/reference/hotkeys) lists them all.
