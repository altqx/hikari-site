The Grid lists every line of the file, one row each, in file order. The line you click becomes the active line, which the [Line editor](/en/docs/basics/line-editor), the video and the audio follow.

## Columns

| Column | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| **#** | The line's number |
| **L.** | Layer: a higher layer is drawn over a lower one |
| **Start**, **End** | The times |
| **Style**, **Actor**, **Effect** | The line's fields |
| **Left**, **Right**, **Vert.** | Margins that replace the style's |
| **CPS** | Characters per second |
| **Wraps** | How the text wraps |
| **Text** | The text |

**CPS** turns the warning colour above 15 characters per second, and **Wraps** above 43 characters on a line, or at three lines. Tags and drawings don't count. **File › Settings › Grid › Counting** chooses whether spaces and punctuation do.

Columns that have nothing to show hide themselves: Layer when every layer is 0, Actor and Effect when they are all empty, and each margin when it is 0 everywhere. A column comes back as soon as a line uses it.

Right-click the row of column names for the list of columns: tick or untick one to show or hide it. **Hide empty columns** turns the hiding above on or off, and **Reset column widths** puts the widths back. The same list is under **Columns** in the menu of the **⋯** button on the Grid panel's header.

 SRT and the other simple formats have no layers, styles, actors, margins or effects, so those columns never show for them.

## What the rows show

- **Comment lines**, kept in the file but not shown on the video, have a tinted background.
- A **dot** in the **#** column marks a line changed since the file opened, and a **ring** one changed and saved since.
- A **triangle** marks the line you are editing in the Line editor but haven't applied yet. The row shows your edit as you type.

## Selecting

| Mouse or key | Does |
| --- | --- |
| Click | Makes the line active, and the only one selected |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+click | Adds the line to the selection, or takes it out |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd>+click | Selects from the active line to this one |
| Drag | Selects the rows you pass |
| Double-click | Moves the video to the line's start, or its end in the End column |
| <kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Down</kbd> <kbd>PgUp</kbd> <kbd>PgDn</kbd> <kbd>Home</kbd> <kbd>End</kbd> | Move the active line. With <kbd>Shift</kbd>, select as they go |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>A</kbd> | Selects every line |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Up</kbd> <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Down</kbd> | Previous or next line, from anywhere |
| <kbd>F2</kbd> | Selects the line at the video's time |

The Grid's keys work while the pointer is over it, without clicking it first. To make a single click move the video too, change **File › Settings › Video › Playback › Move video to the selected line**. **Edit › Select lines** selects lines by their text, style or other fields.

Most commands act on the selected lines.

## The right-click menu

Right-click a line, or press <kbd>Menu</kbd> or <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>F10</kbd>, for the Grid's menu:

- **Insert ›** Before or After, also at the video's time or frame time.
- **Duplicate**, **Cut**, **Copy** and **Paste**.
- **Paste special ›** Copy columns, Paste columns, and [Paste translation from a file…](/en/docs/translation/line-matching#paste-a-translation-from-a-file).
- **Join ›** Join, Keep first or Keep last.
- **Split ›** At video time, Into frames and its **Bake animations** switch, Into characters, Into words or By wraps.
- [Interpolate across lines…](#interpolating-across-lines), for three or more lines.
- **Swap lines**, **Make times continuous ›** with the previous or the next line, **Make line group** and [Convert Aegisub folds to line groups](#aegisub-folds).
- **Select lines visible on video**, **Show subtitles preview** and [Translation matching…](/en/docs/translation/line-matching#line-up-the-translation).
- **Delete text**, and last, **Delete** with the number of selected lines, such as **Delete 3 lines**. **Duplicate** names the number too.

The sections below explain each of them.

## Making and removing lines

- **Insert › Insert before** and **Insert after** add an empty line beside the active one, with its style and fields, timed to fit the gap or 4 seconds long. **Insert before with video time** and the other video items start it at the video's time.
- **Duplicate lines** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>D</kbd>) copies the selected lines, and selects the copies.
- **Swap** swaps two selected lines.
- **Delete lines** (<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Del</kbd>) deletes the selected lines. **Delete text** (<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Del</kbd>) empties their text and keeps their times.

## Joining and splitting

- **Join lines** makes the selected lines one, from the earliest start to the latest end, their texts joined with line breaks.
- **Join lines and keep first** and **Join lines and keep last** keep only one line's text.
- <kbd>F4</kbd> and <kbd>F5</kbd> join the active line with the line before or after it.

Lines between the selected ones that you didn't select are left alone.

**Split lines** cuts a line at the video's time, or into one line per frame, character, word or wrapped line. A split into characters, words or wraps gives each piece its own position, so it stays where it was on the video.

A split into characters keeps each character together with its marks: Thai vowel and tone marks, combining accents and emoji sequences stay with the character they belong to. So `กินน้ำ` splits into `กิ`, `น` and `น้ำ`, an `é` typed as `e` and an accent stays whole, and an emoji, even one made of several joined emoji, is one line. Thai support is by Jibrill.

### Baking animations

**Split › Into frames** needs a video. It makes one line for each frame the line is on, and with **Split › Bake animations** ticked, which it is at first, each frame's line shows the line as it looks on that frame:

- `\t` tags give the values they have reached by that frame, with their acceleration. Sizes, colours, alphas and rectangular clips move between their values. A tag that can't be part way, such as `\an`, takes effect once its `\t` has finished.
- `\fad` and `\fade` are worked into each frame's alphas.
- `\move` becomes the `\pos` for that frame.

Untick **Bake animations** for the split from before: every frame keeps its `\t`, `\fad` and `\fade` tags, and only `\move` becomes `\pos`. The menu item and **File › Settings › Grid › Splitting › Bake animations when splitting into frames** are the same setting. Either way, the whole split is one undo step.

## Interpolating across lines

**Interpolate across lines…** fills the lines between the first and the last selected line with the tag values between theirs, so a sign can move, turn or change colour from one line to the next. It needs at least three selected lines, such as the lines **Split › Into frames** makes. Lines you didn't select, and hidden lines, aren't part of the run.

Each middle line gets values by its place in the run. With `{\pos(0,0)}` on the first of three lines and `{\pos(100,50)}` on the last, the middle line gets `{\pos(50,25)}`. It works on:

- numbers such as sizes, scales, borders and rotations;
- colours and alphas, channel by channel;
- `\pos` and `\org`;
- rectangle and drawing `\clip` and `\iclip`. A drawing moves point by point, so both ends need the same commands and number of points;
- `\t` tags written the same way at both ends, with the same tags inside: their times, acceleration and values.

Only the tags whose values differ between the first and last line change. In each middle line, the tag's old values go, from its `\t` tags too, and the new value is written. Other tags, and the text, are left alone. A tag that one end doesn't have takes the value an earlier tag block gives it, or else the style's. A missing `\pos` takes the start of a `\move`, or else where the line's alignment puts it. A tag keeps the spelling the ends give it, so `\1c` stays `\1c` and `\fr` stays `\fr`.

Tags in the middle of the text count too: they are matched by the characters they come before. When the first and last lines have different text, only the tags at the start are interpolated, and the window says so.

The window lists every tag that changes, with its value on the first and last line, each ticked. Untick one to leave it as it is. A clip or `\t` that can't be interpolated is greyed, with the reason, such as *The clips have different shapes.* Below the list:

- **Acceleration** is 1 for even steps. Above 1 starts slow and ends fast; below 1 starts fast and ends slow.
- **Rotate the shortest way** takes the shorter way round the circle. From `\frz350` to `\frz10`, the middle line gets `\frz180` without it and `\frz0` with it.
- **Cascade transforms** runs each line's `\t` tags one after another over the line. On a one-second line, `{\t(\fscx200)\t(0,5,2,\frz30)}` becomes `{\t(0,500,\fscx200)\t(500,1000,2,\frz30)}`. It re-times the first and last lines' `\t` tags too.
- **Result** shows each line's new text.

While the window is open, the video shows the result, and choosing other lines works it out again. **Apply** or <kbd>Enter</kbd> makes the change as one undo step; **Cancel** or <kbd>Esc</kbd> changes nothing. It has no key at first: <kbd>Shift</kbd>+click it in the menu to give it one.

## Copying and pasting

<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>C</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>X</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd> copy, cut and paste whole lines. Pasted lines go before the first selected line.

**Copy columns** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>C</kbd>) copies only the fields you tick, such as the times or the text without tags. **Paste columns** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd>) pastes them into the same fields of the selected lines, in order. Use them to copy the timing of one set of lines onto another.

## Sorting

**Edit › Sort all lines** and **Sort selected lines** order the lines by start time, end time, style, actor, effect, layer or text. Lines that tie keep their order. Sort selected lines moves the selected lines only among their own rows.

There are two text orders:

- **Text** sorts by the whole text, tags included.
- **Text without tags** leaves out the `{…}` blocks, so lines sort by their words.

Each sort is one undo step. None of them has a key at first; give one in **File › Settings › Shortcuts**.

## Hiding lines

Hidden lines are still in the file; the Grid just leaves them out, and most commands skip them.

The filters are under **Filtering**, in the menu of the **⋯** button on the Grid panel's header:

- **Hide selected lines (filter)** hides them.
- **Hide comments** and **Hide lines with styles ›** hide those lines, and **Show unconfirmed** and **Show untranslated** show only those. **Reverse filtering** shows the opposite, and **Turn off filtering** shows them all again.
- The marks at the left edge show where hidden lines are. Click one to open or close that run.
- **Ignore filtering in some actions**, in the same **⋯** menu, lets Replace all and Shift times reach hidden lines too.

**View › Hide tags** shows tags in the Text column as a single ☀, so the words are easier to read. **File › Settings › Grid › ASS tag replacement** sets the character.

## Line groups

**Make line group** groups the selected lines under a description row that opens and closes them like a folder. Right-click the description row, or press <kbd>Menu</kbd> on it, for **Select the group's lines**, **Add 3 selected lines** (naming how many are selected), **Copy line group**, **Change description…**, **Convert Aegisub folds to line groups** and, apart, **Ungroup**. An edit that would split a group's lines apart asks first, or is refused.

### Groups inside groups

Line groups can sit inside other line groups:

- Select lines inside a group and choose **Make line group**, and the new group sits inside that group. Select whole groups, and the new group takes them in.
- Each level is indented a little further in the Text column. Every group opens and closes on its own, and closing a group hides everything inside it, inner groups included.
- **Ungroup** removes only that group: what was inside moves up one level.
- **Select the group's lines** opens the group and selects everything inside, inner groups too.
- **Copy line group** copies the group with everything inside. A pasted group goes in at the level where you paste it.
- **Add 3 selected lines** works at the group's own level. A selected group's description row brings its whole group, which then sits inside.

Each of these is one undo step.

In the file, a line keeps one `[tree_opened]` or `[tree_closed]` marker in its Actor field for each group it is inside, outermost first. The Line editor and scripts see the Actor without them. A file with no group inside another saves exactly as before. HikariSub 0.0.x opens a file with nested groups as flat groups, and if it saves the file, the nesting is lost.

### Aegisub folds

Folds made in arch1t3cht's Aegisub can become line groups. Opening a file that has them shows *This file has 3 Aegisub folds.*, with **Convert to line groups**. **Convert Aegisub folds to line groups** in the Grid's right-click menu does the same at any time; it has no key at first.

- Each fold becomes a line group, and folds inside folds become groups inside groups. A collapsed fold becomes a closed group.
- Before each group, a comment line holds the fold's first line without its tags, as the group's description.
- Folds that overlap a line group, and fold marks without a partner, are left as they are. A notice says how many folds were converted and skipped.
- It is one undo step.

Opened in Aegisub again, the converted folds no longer show as folds; the skipped ones still do.

## Folding generated lines

The [karaoke templater](/en/docs/karaoke/templater) writes its lines with the Effect `fx`, and Kara Effector writes `Effector [fx]`. A song can leave hundreds of them in the Grid. To fold them away, turn on **Fold generated lines** in the menu of the **⋯** button on the Grid panel's header, which a right-click on the header also opens. Each run of generated lines in a row then shows as a single row. The [Karaoke layout](/en/docs/customize/layouts#karaoke-mode-and-folding) turns folding on for you. HikariSub remembers the option.

A folded row sits between dashed rules. It shows the line numbers it covers, such as 6–10, the earliest start and latest end, the styles and effects of its lines, and how many lines it holds, such as **5 generated lines**.

- The arrow at the start of its Text unfolds the run, and the arrow on the run's first row folds it again.
- <kbd>Right</kbd> unfolds the run while its row is the active one, and <kbd>Left</kbd> on the run's first row folds it. On these rows the keys fold instead of stepping the video a frame; elsewhere they work as usual.
- Clicking a folded row selects every line of the run, so Delete, Copy and Shift times act on the whole run. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+click takes the run out of the selection.
- Find, or anything else that makes one of a folded run's lines active or selects only part of the run, unfolds it.

Folding only hides rows. The lines stay in the file and play on the video. Unlike [hidden lines](#hiding-lines), they count as selected, and commands act on every line a folded row stands for. A folded row shows while any of its lines passes the filter; the lines the filter hides stay hidden.

Turning the option off shows every line, and turning it on again folds every run.

## Other commands

- **Make times continuous with the previous line** and **with the next line** close the gaps between lines, as [Timing](/en/docs/basics/timing#lines-next-to-each-other) explains.
- **Subtitles › Set FPS › Set new FPS…** and **Set FPS from video** retime lines from one frame rate to another.
- **Select lines visible on video** selects the lines showing at the video's time.
- **Show subtitles preview** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Q</kbd>) opens another file beside this one, to compare or translate against.
- **Subtitles › Font collector** gathers the fonts the file uses.
- **File › Import subtitles from MKV…** opens a video's subtitle track.

Every Grid command has a place in the [Shortcuts reference](/en/docs/reference/hotkeys#grid), and most have no key at first. **Shift**+click a menu item to give it one.
