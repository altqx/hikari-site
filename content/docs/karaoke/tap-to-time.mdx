Tap to time times a karaoke line by ear. While the song plays, press <kbd>Space</kbd> as each syllable is sung, and HikariSub writes the line's `\k` tags from your taps. Each line you finish is applied, and tapping goes on with the next line without stopping the song.

There are two ways to tap, set in the Tap menu under **Each syllable**:

- **Hold the key: releasing it leaves a gap.** This is the default. Pressing <kbd>Space</kbd> starts a syllable and releasing it ends it. The time between a release and the next press becomes a gap, written as an empty syllable.
- **Press the key: no gaps.** Each press starts the next syllable and ends the one before.

Your first press sets the line's start, and the end of its last syllable sets the line's end. The syllables come from the line's split: see [Split and join](/en/docs/karaoke/split-and-join). Times are measured in the song, so tapping at a slower speed gives the same result.

## Starting

Tap to time works in the Audio panel's **Karaoke** mode, with audio open. The [Karaoke layout](/en/docs/customize/layouts) turns the mode on. Then:

- Click the **Tap** button, after **Auto split** in the Audio panel's button row. It stays pressed while you tap.
- Or press <kbd>Y</kbd> while the Audio panel has the keyboard.
- Or choose **Start tapping** in the menu of Tap's arrow, **Tap options**.

When the panel is narrow, Tap folds into the **⋯** menu as **Tap to time** and **Tap options**. <kbd>Shift</kbd>+click the Tap button to give it another key.

Tapping starts on the active line. The Audio panel takes the keyboard, and the audio plays from one second before the line's start to the end of the audio.

## The tap bar

While tap mode is on, a bar over the waveform shows, from the left:

- The state: **Tapping** with a red dot, **Stopped** with a square, or **Ready** with a ring.
- **Line** and the line's number.
- The line's syllables. Tapped ones are dimmed, the one you are holding is filled, and the next one is outlined.
- After **›**, the next line's text.
- A message, when there is one.
- On the right, what you can do next: **Undo** and **Stop** while tapping, **Resume** and **Revert Line** when stopped.

On the waveform, each tapped syllable shows as a block with its text, under the letters. The syllable you are holding grows at the playhead, and a gap is hatched.

## Keys

While tap mode is on, the Audio panel takes <kbd>Space</kbd>, <kbd>Backspace</kbd> and <kbd>Esc</kbd> before any other key, including the video's <kbd>Space</kbd>. Held keys don't repeat.

| Key | Tapping | Ready or Stopped |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <kbd>Space</kbd> | Taps: see above | Starts, or resumes |
| <kbd>Backspace</kbd> | Takes back the syllable you are holding and the last tapped one, and plays again from two seconds before | The same, without playing |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Backspace</kbd> | Takes back the whole line's taps, and plays from two seconds before the line | The same |
| <kbd>Esc</kbd> or <kbd>H</kbd> | Stops, keeping the taps | <kbd>Esc</kbd> leaves tap mode |
| <kbd>Y</kbd> | Leaves tap mode | Leaves tap mode |
| <kbd>[</kbd> <kbd>]</kbd> | Slower, faster | Slower, faster |

Only <kbd>Space</kbd> taps. While you are tapping, the Audio panel's other keys wait, and so do its Commit, Previous line, Next line, Add lead-in and Add lead-out buttons and the syllable commands. If the Audio panel loses the keyboard while you hold <kbd>Space</kbd>, for example because you click in the Grid, the syllable ends at that moment.

## How taps become times

- A tap stands for the playing position, less the calibrated latency, rounded to 10 ms.
- A gap shorter than 30 ms joins the syllable before it.
- A syllable lasts at least 10 ms.
- Time never runs backwards: a press before the last boundary, for example while the audio replays after an undo, lands on that boundary.
- Each syllable keeps its own kind of tag, `\k`, `\kf` or `\ko`. A gap becomes an empty syllable with the tag of the syllable before it.

For example, tapping *ki*, *mi* and *no* (a `\kf`) with a short gap after *mi*:

```
Taps     ki 1.20–1.50 s, mi 1.50–1.80 s, no 1.90–2.25 s
Line     0:00:01.20 to 0:00:02.25
Text     {\k30}ki{\k30}mi{\k10}{\kf35}no
```

In Hold mode a line is done when you release its last syllable. In Press mode it is done at the press after the last syllable's start; that press also starts the next line's first syllable.

Switching between Hold and Press while you hold a syllable drops that syllable.

## Line after line

Each finished line is applied as one step named *Tap timing*. The next line becomes active, the bar shows its syllables, and you go on tapping while the song plays. After the last line the audio stops and the bar says *Every Line is tapped.*

Choose another line while tapping, in the Grid or with Previous line and Next line, and tapping starts on that line afresh, one second before it.

If a line can't take the new times, for example because the Line editor holds an edit that isn't valid, nothing is written. The audio stops and the bar says *Not applied: the Line cannot take these times as it is.*

When the audio reaches its end, a syllable you are holding ends there.

## Stopping and correcting

- **Stop**, <kbd>Esc</kbd>, drops a syllable you are holding, keeps the tapped ones and stops the audio. The bar says how far you got, such as *3 of 5 tapped*.
- **Resume**, or <kbd>Space</kbd>, plays again from two seconds before the end of the last tapped syllable, or from one second before the line if nothing is tapped.
- **Revert Line** drops every tap on the line. The line itself is unchanged, since nothing was written yet.
- <kbd>Backspace</kbd> takes back the syllable you are holding, if any, and the last tapped one, and says which, such as *Undid “mi”*. The audio plays again from two seconds before the earliest of them. <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Backspace</kbd> takes back the whole line and says *Undid the Line*. Neither goes back before the start of the audio.

These keys only change the line you are tapping. Lines already applied are undone with **Edit › Undo**: each is one *Tap timing* step.

## Leaving tap mode

Press <kbd>Y</kbd> or the Tap button again, choose **Stop tapping**, or press <kbd>Esc</kbd> when the bar says Ready or Stopped. The line you were tapping keeps its old timing, and the audio stops. Leaving Karaoke mode, or closing or replacing the audio, leaves tap mode too.

## What you tap

Tap to time taps the syllables of the line's split, with two changes:

- Empty syllables are skipped, so tapping a line again replaces the gaps of the last time.
- A syllable of only spaces joins the syllable before it.

A line of one syllable can't be tapped: a tap would only set its start and end. The bar says *This Line is one syllable. Split it first, then tap.* Press **Auto split** on the bar to split it, then <kbd>Space</kbd> to start.

## Slower playback

Play the song at 25, 33, 50, 75 or 100% while you tap; the pitch stays the same. The speed applies to everything the Audio panel plays, in Line mode too, and HikariSub remembers it.

- In the **Speed** submenu of the Tap menu.
- In the Audio panel's speaker menu, **Volume and output device**, on the **Speed** row.
- With <kbd>[</kbd> for **Slower** and <kbd>]</kbd> for **Faster**, which work while you tap.

When the speed is below 100%, the speaker button shows it, such as *50*.

## Calibrating the latency

Between the moment you hear a syllable and the moment your tap arrives, there is a small delay, from your reaction and from your sound device. Measure it once, and HikariSub takes it off every tap.

Choose **Calibrate latency…** in the Tap menu; the menu shows the current value. The **Calibrate tap latency** window plays 16 beeps at 100 BPM at once. Press <kbd>Space</kbd> on each beep. The first four are practice and don't count.

Each press is shown on the strip by how many milliseconds it came after its beep. Presses far from the others are left out, and the **Latency** is the middle value of the rest. Once six beeps have counted, the field takes the result, unless you have typed a value yourself. The note beside it gives the spread.

**Apply**, or <kbd>Enter</kbd>, keeps the value. **Cancel**, or <kbd>Esc</kbd>, leaves the latency as it was, and **Start again** plays the beeps again. You can also type a value from −200 to 500 ms when the beeps have stopped. The latency starts at 0 ms.
