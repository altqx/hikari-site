title: The Agent panel
description: Ask HikariSub's AI agent to work on your subtitles in threads, choose how much it may do without asking, and cap what it costs.

The **Agent** panel is where you work with HikariSub's AI agent. You ask in a thread, a conversation of its own, and the agent reads your subtitles and changes them. Every change it makes to your subtitles waits in the [Review panel](/en/docs/ai/review) until you keep or undo it.

HikariSub has no AI server of its own. The agent runs on a provider you add, with your API key, a ChatGPT or OpenRouter sign-in, or a model on this computer. A thread can also run on an agent you have installed, [Claude Code or Codex](#agents-on-this-computer).

## Opening the panel

Open it from **View › Panels › Agent**. It opens as a tab beside the Review panel, or to the right of the Grid. <kbd>F1</kbd> in the panel opens this page.

Two commands have no key at first; give them one in **File › Settings › Shortcuts**:

| Command | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Agent panel** | Shows the panel |
| **New agent thread** | Shows the panel with a new thread, as its **+** does |

With **Turn off all AI features** ticked in **File › Settings › AI**, the panel and both commands are gone.

Until you add a provider with a chat model, or install Claude Code or Codex, the panel says *Add a provider to use the agent*, with **Open Settings › AI**. Nothing is sent or downloaded until then. The agent uses the **Chat** model in **File › Settings › AI › Models**. With no Chat model, a new thread runs on an [agent on this computer](#agents-on-this-computer) instead: Claude Code if you have it, otherwise Codex.

## Asking

Type in the box at the foot of the panel and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>, or **Send**. <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd> starts a new line.

- **What the agent sees.** The chip above the box names the active tab, as in *Episode 01.ass · active tab*. The agent reads that tab, and the tabs you attach. Type `@`, or press **Attach**, to attach another open tab; click its chip to take it off again. For a tab you haven't attached, the agent asks you to attach it.
- **Commands.** Type `/` for the list: `/new` starts a new thread, and `/mode` opens the mode menu.
- **Stopping.** While the agent works, <kbd>Esc</kbd> in the box, or **Stop**, stops the turn. A tool already running finishes first. The thread says *You stopped the turn.*
- **Sending while it works.** A message you send while the agent works waits in a strip above the box, *Queued ·* and your message, and is sent when the turn ends. **Steer now** gives it to the agent at once, after its next step, and marks it *steered*. **Edit** puts it back in the box, and **Remove** drops it.

Below the box, the model button picks the thread's model from your providers' models, or an [agent on this computer](#agents-on-this-computer), and the mode button its [mode](#modes).

## What the thread shows

- **Your messages,** with their time and **Restore checkpoint**, which goes back to before that turn. See [Checkpoints](/en/docs/ai/review#checkpoints).
- **The agent's answers,** as they come.
- **Each tool the agent used,** such as reading lines. Click one to see what it asked for and what it got.
- **Each change it made,** with **Review**, which opens the Review panel. In Auto mode, a change the judge approved says *judge approved*.
- **Its plan,** for longer work: a list it ticks off as it goes.
- **Proposals,** in Manual mode: the subtitle edits the agent wants to make, for you to apply or decline. See [Proposals](#proposals).
- **Questions** for you, and notices, such as *The turn failed* with **Retry the turn**.
- **Searches,** citations and sources, when the agent [searched the web](#searching-the-web).

## Skills

Skills are instructions for kinds of work, which the agent reads when your request matches one. Three come with HikariSub: **typesetting**, **translation** and **karaoke-templater**. Each thread sees every skill's name and description. It reads a skill's instructions when the work calls for it, and the skill's other files only when it needs them.

Skills are text only. They run nothing and give the agent no permission: what it does after reading one asks you as anything else would, by the thread's [mode](#modes). With **Turn off all AI features** ticked, nothing reads them.

### Adding your own

Put a folder with a `SKILL.md` in `ai/skills` in your [user folder](/en/docs/automation/user-folder#whats-in-it). Skills use the [Agent Skills format](https://agentskills.io/specification), so a skill written for another agent works here too.

```md
---
name: sign-colours
description: Matching sign colours to the video. Use it when the user asks to colour a sign like the one on screen.
---

# Sign colours

1. Take the colour from the frame under the sign.
2. …
```

- **The name** is the folder's name: lowercase letters and digits, in words joined by hyphens, up to 64 characters.
- **The description** says what the skill is for and when to use it, in up to 1,024 characters. The agent picks skills by their descriptions alone.
- **Other files** in the folder, such as `references/terms.md`, are read when the agent needs them. They are read as text: nothing in a skill runs.

A skill of yours with a built-in skill's name replaces it. A new or changed skill shows in the next thread. HikariSub reads up to 100 skills, 100 files in each, four folders deep and 256 KiB a file, and skips hidden files and symbolic links. A skill that breaks the format, such as one without a description, is left out.

Skills are for threads on a provider. A thread on [Claude Code](#agents-on-this-computer) and agents connected through MCP don't get them.

## Typesetting signs

Ask the agent to typeset a sign, and it follows the built-in **typesetting** skill. It looks at the sign's frame without subtitles, takes the sign's colours, and reads where its corners are. It doesn't work out the placement tags itself: HikariSub's own [visual tools](/en/docs/typesetting/visual-tools) write them.

- **Perspective** puts a line on the sign's four corners, with `\pos`, `\org`, the rotations, `\fax` and the scales.
- The clip and drawing tools write a rectangle or vector clip, or put a shape.
- The **Position shifter** moves positions, `\move` points, clips and `\org` together, for a sign that moved.
- **Hydra** sets, adds to, multiplies or runs a gradient of one tag across lines, from its own list of tags.

The agent writes the other tags, such as the font, colours and borders.

Before anything changes, each line is drawn alone on its frame, and where its ink lands is compared with where the sign is. A line passes when every edge is within 4 video pixels. The agent places up to 100 lines at a time, and only when every one passes: if one misses, none of them is applied, and the agent is told which. Lines that pass go in as one undo step, and wait in the [review](/en/docs/ai/review) for **Keep** or **Undo**.

Placing the lines asks as other changes do in the thread's [mode](#modes). In Manual mode it is a [question](#questions), not a proposal, and in Auto the judge decides. Typesetting needs an open video.

## Searching the web

With **File › Settings › AI › Let AI search the web** on, the globe beside **Attach** turns web search on or off for the thread. It starts on, and the thread keeps your choice. Its hover says *Search the web: on for this thread* or *off for this thread*. On Anthropic, it adds that changing it partway through a thread costs one cache miss.

When the thread's provider can't search on its own and no search service is chosen, the globe is dimmed. Its hover says *This provider can't search the web on its own. Choose a search service in Settings › AI › Web search.* With **Let AI search the web** off, the globe is hidden.

- **Search rows.** Each search shows in the thread as *Searched the web*, *Searched with Google* or *Searched with* and the service, such as *Searched with Brave Search*, followed by what it searched for. A page the agent read shows as *Read* and the site. Expand a row to see the full addresses.
- **Citations.** Numbered chips follow the text they back up. Hover one for the page's title, its site and, when the provider sends one, the quoted passage, with **Open in browser**.
- **Sources.** Under an answer that cites pages, **Sources** lists them, each title a link with its site. After five, the rest fold behind **Show 2 more**.
- **Google's suggestions.** Under a Gemini answer that searched, Google's search suggestions show as Google draws them. Click one to search for it in your browser.
- **A search service that stops.** When the search service's plan is used up, the thread says so, such as *Brave Search has no searches left*. When it refuses the key, it says *Brave Search refused HikariSub's key*. Under either, it gives the service's answer, such as *The search service answered 429. The answer goes on without searching the web.* **Open Settings › AI** opens the settings.

Only `http` and `https` links open, always in your browser. What a search sends, and what the thread keeps, is in [What is sent](/en/docs/help/what-is-sent#ai).

## Agents on this computer

A thread can run on an AI agent you have installed instead of on a provider: Claude Code, or on Linux, Codex. When `claude` is on your `PATH`, the model menu lists **Claude Code** first. When `codex` is, it lists **Codex** after it. You don't need a provider for either.

If you installed Claude Code or Codex with mise or asdf, the `claude` or `codex` on your `PATH` is the version manager's shim. HikariSub looks past it to the real program: one further down your `PATH`, or else the one mise or asdf says it runs. If it can't find one, the agent counts as not installed. Start HikariSub from a terminal to see why: it prints that it found a shim it couldn't resolve.

### Claude Code

A thread on Claude Code runs on your own `claude`, with its sign-in and its default model:

- **Only HikariSub's tools.** It works through the same tools as any thread. Claude Code's own tools, such as running commands or editing files, are off, and your Claude Code settings and MCP servers aren't loaded.
- **The review as usual.** Its edits wait in the [Review panel](/en/docs/ai/review) until you keep or undo them.
- **Costs.** The cost Claude Code reports for each turn counts toward the thread's and the day's [cost caps](#cost-caps).
- **When it fails.** If a turn fails, its error ends with what `claude` printed, after *Claude said:*.

The first time you use Claude Code, HikariSub downloads the Claude Agent SDK, which it uses to talk to `claude`. See [What is sent](/en/docs/help/what-is-sent#ai).

### Codex

A thread on Codex runs on your own `codex`, signed in with HikariSub's own ChatGPT sign-in: **Sign in with ChatGPT…** in **File › Settings › AI › External agents**. HikariSub keeps it apart from the sign-in in `~/.codex`, so neither signs the other out. Without it, Codex doesn't start. Codex is on Linux only for now; Windows will follow.

- **Only HikariSub's tools.** It works through the same tools as any thread. HikariSub turns down Codex's own commands, file changes and requests for permission, and Codex doesn't use your `~/.codex` settings.
- **The review as usual.** Its edits wait in the [Review panel](/en/docs/ai/review) until you keep or undo them.
- **Costs.** Codex reports the tokens it used but not a cost, so a Codex thread says *cost not reported* in place of a cost, and doesn't count toward your [cost caps](#cost-caps). Your ChatGPT plan's limits apply instead.

To let Claude Code you start yourself edit your files instead, see [Connect Claude Code or Codex](/en/docs/ai/connect-an-agent).

## Modes

Each thread has a mode, which decides what the agent may change without asking. Reading never asks, and neither does selecting lines or opening, closing and focusing panels.

| Mode | What asks you |
| --- | --- |
| **Manual** | You approve each [subtitle edit](#proposals) before it's made, and every other change. Reading never asks. |
| **Auto** | The judge model approves each change, or asks you, with its reason |
| **Full access** | Nothing, except the [always-ask list](#the-always-ask-list) and [Lua scripts](#lua-scripts) |

New threads start in **Auto**. Change a thread's mode with the mode button, or `/mode`. **File › Settings › AI › Agent › Mode for new threads** sets the mode new threads start in; **Default for new threads…**, at the foot of the mode menu, opens it.

**Full access** shows in red, and the thread's title gets a *Full access* badge. The first time you choose it, HikariSub asks *Turn on Full access?*, and lists what still asks. **Don't warn me again** stops the question.

### The judge

In Auto mode, a model checks each change before it is made: the **Judge** model in **File › Settings › AI › Models**. It sees the change the agent wants to make, including any text it would write, and your own messages, never the files it read or what its tools returned, so nothing the agent read can talk it into approving. If it doesn't approve, or can't answer, you are asked, with *Why:* and its reason. What the judge costs counts toward the thread's cost.

### Questions

When the agent needs your yes, the thread shows a card, such as *Run commands_run?*, with what it wants to do. A card from the judge says *The judge asks you*.

- **Allow once** lets this one action through.
- **Allow in this thread** lets the same action through for the rest of the thread without asking. For a command, it covers that one command.
- **Deny** refuses it, and the agent is told not to try again. **Deny and tell the agent…** adds a message: *What should it do instead?*

A card says what you chose, or *No longer needed* when the turn stopped first.

### Proposals

In Manual mode, the agent's subtitle edits come to you as a proposal in the thread. Nothing in your subtitles changes until you press **Apply**. This goes for Claude Code and Codex threads too: a proposal can wait up to a day, and the agent keeps waiting for your answer.

A proposal card, *Proposed:* and the change's name, has one row for each edit: the line's old text struck through and its new text, or *Inserted*, *Deleted* or *Moved*. The first five rows show, and **Show all** shows the rest. Clicking a row selects its line and applies nothing.

- **Apply** makes the whole change, as one undoable step in the [Review panel](/en/docs/ai/review), where you can still keep or undo it.
- **Decline** writes nothing, and the agent is told not to propose it again unless you ask. **Decline and tell the agent…** adds a message: *What should it do instead?*

A proposal is applied or declined as a whole, never one line at a time, and it has no **Allow in this thread**.

If your subtitles change while a proposal waits, it goes *out of date*: *The subtitles changed after the agent read them.* **Apply** is off. **Ask again** makes the agent read the subtitles again and propose again, or **Decline** drops it.

A new proposal puts the focus on **Apply** only when you are in the Agent panel but not typing in its box, so <kbd>Enter</kbd> in the box never applies one. Stopping the turn, a [cost cap](#cost-caps) or deleting the thread drops a waiting proposal, and nothing is written.

Agents you [connect over MCP](/en/docs/ai/connect-an-agent), plugins and the other AI features don't make proposals: their edits go straight into the review.

### The always-ask list

Some actions ask in every mode, Full access included. Their card has a red edge, asks *Allow …?* and says *asks in every mode*, and offers only **Allow once** and **Deny**:

- **Deleting or overwriting files** outside your subtitles' folders and your [user folder](/en/docs/automation/user-folder), such as the command that deletes temporary files.
- **Running external programs,** such as loading or rerunning an Automation script, plugin commands, and the settings for the script editor and installing updates.
- **Installing plugins or trusting them:** the settings for plugin updates, plugin network access and trusted folders.
- **Sending local files to the network:** every privacy setting, such as crash reports and usage data.
- **Changing AI settings,** such as permissions, keys and budgets.

Saving, exporting and the other commands that ask for a file name aren't on the list: you choose the file yourself.

### Lua scripts

The agent can write an [Automation](/en/docs/automation/running-scripts) script in Lua and run it as a macro. Every run asks first, in every mode, Full access included. The card has a red edge, asks *Run this Lua script?* and says *asks in every mode*. It shows:

- the script's name, the macro it runs if the agent named one, and the tab it runs on;
- the script, with line numbers. The first 12 lines show, and **Show all … lines** shows the rest;
- each [DependencyControl](/en/docs/automation/dependencycontrol) module the script needs, with its version when it is installed. A missing one is marked: *… isn't installed, so the script will stop when it loads.*

**Run** runs it, and **Don't run** doesn't. **Don't run and tell the agent…** adds a message: *What should it do instead?* There is no **Allow in this thread**, and running the same script again asks again.

An approved script runs like any macro, on the active tab's selection: it runs the macro the agent named, or the script's first one. It is one undo step, named *Automation:* and the macro's name, and **Force stop** on the card ends it while it runs. Another macro running, or no open file, stops it before it starts.

HikariSub saves the script in `ai/scripts` in your [user folder](/en/docs/automation/user-folder), under the name the agent gave it, and replaces an earlier one of that name. **Open**, on the card, opens the file. The script is never added to `autoload`, so it doesn't load at start.


## Threads

**Threads**, at the top left, lists your threads by day, with each one's files, model and cost. *running*, *at cap* or *Full access* marks a thread. **Search threads** finds them by title or file name. Click one to open it, and **+** starts a new one.

The thread menu, beside **+**, has:

- **Rename thread…**
- **Export as Markdown…**, which saves the conversation as a `.md` file.
- **Delete thread**, which asks first: *The conversation goes. Changes it made stay in your subtitles and in the review.*

Threads are kept in `ai/threads` in your user folder, so they are still there after a restart, with their mode, model, cost and what you allowed. Images the agent looked at aren't kept.

If the AI component stops during a turn, the thread says *The AI component stopped*. Changes the turn made stay in the review, and the turn doesn't run again by itself: **Retry the turn** sends your message again, and **Show the log** opens the Log.

## Cost caps

Each thread has a cost cap, $2.00 at first, and so does each day, $10.00, counted from what the providers report for each request. At a cap, the turn stops after the step it is on, and any open question closes.

- **A thread at its cap** says *This thread reached its $2.00 cap*. **Raise to $4.00 and continue** raises this thread's cap by the cap for new threads, and tells the agent to go on.
- **The day's cap** says *Today's $10.00 cap is reached*. Every thread stops until tomorrow, or until you raise the cap in **File › Settings › AI › Agent**.

The agent never raises a cap; only you can. A model that reports no cost and has no price shows *cost not reported*, and doesn't count toward a cap.

The meter above the box shows how much of the model's context the thread uses, such as *12.4K of 200K tokens*, and its cost against its cap, such as *$0.42 of $2.00*, in red once it's reached.

## Settings

**File › Settings › AI › Agent**:

| Row | At first | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mode for new threads** | Auto | Manual, Auto or Full access |
| **Cost cap per thread** | $2.00 | A new thread's cap, in steps of $0.50. 0 is *No cap* |
| **Cost cap per day** | $10.00 | The cap for every thread together, each day. 0 is *No cap* |
| **Show the cost meter** | On | Shows the meter above the box |

Changes to the caps don't touch threads you already have, except the day's cap, which counts for every thread.

What HikariSub sends to providers is in [What is sent](/en/docs/help/what-is-sent#ai).
