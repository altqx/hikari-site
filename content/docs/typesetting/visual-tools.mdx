The tools sit in the rail on the left of the Video panel. Click one, or press its key while the pointer is over the video: <kbd>P</kbd> for Text positioning, <kbd>R</kbd> for Z rotation. Choosing the tool that is already on goes back to the Position pointer. The rail's checked button is the tool that is on; hover it for its name. When the Video panel is too short for every tool, the rail shows the ones that fit and ends in **More tools**, a menu of the rest. The tool that is on always stays in view.

3. **The inspector bar:** the tool's options for the active line, on one line. When they don't fit, the last groups fold under **More options**, where typing or scrubbing a value works at once, as on the bar. The bar has the same height for every tool, so switching tools never resizes the video.

**File › Settings › Video › Visual tools › Handle size**, from 50 to 400 %, makes every tool's handles bigger or smaller, and how close you need to be to grab them.

## The tools

| Tool | Key | Writes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Position pointer | <kbd>X</kbd> | Nothing; shows the script coordinates under the pointer |
| Text positioning | <kbd>P</kbd> | `\pos` |
| Text moving | <kbd>G</kbd> | `\move` |
| Text scaling | <kbd>S</kbd> | `\fscx` `\fscy` |
| Text Z rotation | <kbd>R</kbd> | `\frz`, and `\org` |
| Text X / Y rotation | <kbd>T</kbd> | `\frx` `\fry`, and `\org` |
| Perspective | <kbd>Q</kbd> | `\pos` `\org` `\frx` `\fry` `\frz` `\fax` `\fscx` `\fscy` and the border and shadow sizes |
| Rectangle clipping | <kbd>C</kbd> | `\clip` `\iclip` |
| Vector clipping | <kbd>V</kbd> | `\clip` with a drawing |
| Vector drawing | <kbd>D</kbd> | a `\p` drawing |
| Position shifter | <kbd>A</kbd> | Moves the positions, `\move` points, clips, drawings and `\org` already in the lines |
| Hydra | <kbd>H</kbd> | Any tag from its list |
| Gradient | <kbd>W</kbd> | Copies of the line in `\clip` strips, each with its own values; a `\p` mask drawing; or tags for each character or line |
| Text on path | None at first | A line for each letter, with `\an2`, `\pos` and `\frz`, along a path |

The [ASS override tags](/en/docs/reference/ass-tags) reference explains each tag.

## Which lines a tool edits

A tool edits the **active line**, the one in the Line editor. To edit several lines at once, select them in the Grid and press **Pick selected lines** at the right end of the inspector bar; the tools then edit the picked lines, not the active line, until you press **Clear picked lines**. Selecting lines in the Grid is not enough on its own.

The tools need the line on screen. While the active line is not at the video's time, the video says *Line is not visible on video or has zero duration* and the tools wait. Most tools leave comment lines alone; Vector drawing and Vector clipping work on them.

When a line has no `\pos`, the handles start where the line would show, from its alignment and margins.

## Using each tool

- **Position pointer:** a cross follows the pointer with its coordinates. It has no inspector bar: its **X**, **Y** and **Copy video position** sit at the end of the video's times line, and **Copy video position** copies them. Ctrl+click or middle-click puts `\pos` there. Only with this tool does a right-click open its menu and a double-click go full screen.
- **Text positioning:** drag anywhere on the video to move the line; you need not grab it. Shift locks to one axis. A right-click puts the line at the pointer. **Set position by rectangle** places it by a rectangle you draw instead, using the alignment you choose.
- **Text moving:** the left button sets the start point and the right button the end point. The bar's **Start** and **End** fields include the times, in milliseconds from the line's start. **Set movement using 2 points and the video position** builds the move from a point on two frames.
- **Text scaling:** left-drag scales the width, right-drag the height, and middle-drag or Shift+drag both. **Set scale by rectangle** scales the line to fit a rectangle.
- **Text Z rotation:** drag around the origin cross to turn the line; drag the cross to move `\org`. **Set angle from 2 points** takes the angle from two points you click along something in the picture.
- **Text X / Y rotation:** left-drag turns about the vertical axis (`\fry`), right-drag about the horizontal axis (`\frx`), middle-drag both.
- **Rectangle clipping:** drag a rectangle, then drag its edges, corners or inside. **Invert clip** switches between `\clip` and `\iclip`.
- **Vector clipping and Vector drawing:** the bar's modes are **Move points**, **Add line**, **Add Bézier curve**, **Add B-spline**, **Add separate point** and **Delete point**; the wheel steps through them. Ctrl+click selects several points, and Alt while dragging snaps to another point's x or y. Vector drawing's **Shape** list draws a ready-made shape into a rectangle you drag; **Edit shapes…** adds your own. Vector drawing can also combine and grow drawings: see [Combining and offsetting drawings](#combining-and-offsetting-drawings). Vector clip points keep two decimals. The vector clip modes and its **Invert clip** can take keys in **File › Settings › Shortcuts**.
- **Position shifter:** drag to move everything the **Move** toggles choose (positions, `\move` start or end points, clips, drawings, `\org`) by the same amount in every line. Or type an amount in the bar's **X** and **Y** fields: each <kbd>Enter</kbd> shifts the lines by that much, as one step.
- **Hydra:** pick a tag, then drag sideways on the video or turn the wheel to change its value. **Set**, **Add**, **Multiply** and **Gradient** decide how the value is applied across characters or lines.
- **Gradient:** the button before Text on path on the rail. Choose a mode, drag across the sign from where the gradient starts to where it ends, check the preview and press **Apply gradient (Enter)**. See [Gradients](#gradients).
- **Text on path:** the last button on the rail. Give the line a vector clip, or draw a path, and press **Apply**. See [Text on path](#text-on-path).

The tools work only on ASS files.

## Nudging with the arrow keys

While the pointer is over the video, the arrow keys move what the tool edits by one script pixel, and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+arrows by a tenth. With Text moving, <kbd>Alt</kbd>+arrows move the end point. Text scaling's arrows change the scale by 1%.

The arrows nudge with Text positioning, Text moving, Text scaling, Perspective, both clipping tools, Vector drawing and the Position shifter. In Vector clipping and Vector drawing they move the selected points.

<Callout>
  Over the video, the arrows nudge instead of stepping frames. Move the pointer to the Grid to step frames with them again. With the Position pointer, the rotations, Hydra and Gradient, the arrows step frames as usual.
</Callout>

## Fitting text to a surface

With Perspective (<kbd>Q</kbd>), drag the corners of the quad onto the surface the text should sit on. HikariSub works out the rotation, shear and origin, and the inspector bar shows the values as you drag.

- Shift while dragging a corner keeps it on one axis of the plane, Ctrl snaps it to another corner's x or y, and Alt scales the quad from its centre.
- Drag inside the quad to move it, or double-click to bring the nearest corner to the pointer.
- **Show surrounding plane** adds an outer quad: match it to a rectangle in the shot, such as a sign's edges, then place the text inside it. The plane is saved with the line. **Show grid** draws a grid over it.
- **Origin** sets where `\org` goes: **Centre** of the quad, **No \fax** for lines with line breaks, or **Keep** to leave it where it is and drag it yourself.

Perspective needs a line that one box can describe. It does nothing for lines that change size or font partway through, wrap, or animate their shape.

## Gradients

Gradient (<kbd>W</kbd>) does in one tool what the gradient scripts do, without leaving the video. The bundled gradient scripts still ship, so you can keep using them.

### Modes

The modes are icon toggles at the start of the inspector bar:

- **Rectangle strips** cuts the line into strips, each in a rectangle `\clip`. They run across your drag, horizontal or vertical, whichever is nearer.
- **Rotated strips** does the same at the angle of your drag.
- **Skewed strips** tilts the strips' edges by **Skew**, in degrees, or with the handle. A 4-point vector clip on the line sets where it starts.
- **Strips along the clip** makes rings inward from the line's clip. It needs no drag.
- **Wedge strips** turns the line into a mask drawing with `\p`. **Shape** picks **Wedge**, **Box**, **Ring**, **Star** or **Vignette**. Place it with the **P1**, **P2** and **P3** handles, or put a 3-point vector clip on the line first.
- **By character** puts a tag block before each character, so the values change along the text. A Thai character cluster or an emoji counts as one character.
- **By line** changes each picked line by its place among them.

The first four make strips. Each line you apply them to becomes a comment and is kept, with its strips after it. Wedge strips, By character and By line change the line's text instead.

### Stops

In the strip modes, **Stops** says where the gradient takes its values from:

- **Values:** the gradient runs from **From** to **To** for each tag in it. A colour has **R**, **G** and **B** fields with a swatch. The tag picker adds tags to the gradient. It lists them under **In the gradient**, **Colour and alpha**, **Border, shadow and blur** and **Text**, and the gradient starts with the primary colour. **Remove the tag from the gradient** drops the one shown. **From** starts at the active line's value.
- **Picked lines:** [pick](#which-lines-a-tool-edits) two or more lines with the same text and tag blocks. Each is a stop, in Grid order, and the tags that differ between them are blended. Every stop becomes a comment, and the strips follow the last one.

By character and By line always use **Values**. Wedge strips has no values. Its drawing keeps the line's first tag block, apart from the position, scale, border and shadow tags.

### Shaping the result

| Field | Modes | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Strip** | Strip modes | How wide each strip is, in pixels. 4 at first |
| **Overlap** | Rectangle, Rotated and Skewed strips | How far each strip reaches into the next, in pixels. 0 at first |
| **Accel** | All but Wedge strips | Speeds the change up or slows it down along the gradient; 1 is even |
| **Skew** | Skewed strips | The tilt of the strips' edges, in degrees |
| **Spacing**, **Thickness**, **Spikes**, **Centre**, **Hole** | Wedge strips | The shape's own sizes; each shape shows the ones it uses |

- **Blend** mixes colours in **RGB** or **HSL**.
- **Steps**, in By character, places each character **By width**, from how wide the text before it is, or **By count**, evenly.
- **Merge equal strips**, on at first, joins neighbouring strips that come out the same, so the file gets fewer lines.

### Applying

Drag across the sign. The modes without a drag show the result at once. The result previews over the video, and the handles and fields still change it until you apply.

**Apply gradient (Enter)**, or <kbd>Enter</kbd> over the video, writes it as one step named **Gradient** in the history. The new lines are selected. <kbd>Esc</kbd> drops the preview, and nothing is written.

When a gradient can't be made, the video says why:

- *Choose a tag for the gradient*
- *Pick two or more lines as stops*
- *The picked lines must have the same text and tag blocks*
- *No tag differs between the picked lines*
- *Drag from where the gradient starts to where it ends*
- *The line has no text or clip to cover*
- *Strips along the clip need a clip on the line*
- *Gradients do not work on an inverse clip*
- *No characters on the line*

## Combining and offsetting drawings

While you draw points yourself in Vector drawing, not a ready-made shape, two more tools follow the **Shape** list on its bar.

**Pathfinder** combines the drawing with a second shape: **Unite**, **Subtract**, **Intersect** or **Exclude**.

- **The second shape** is the line's vector clip, `\clip` or `\iclip`, and the clip comes out once it's used.
- **Without a clip**, it is the drawings of the other [picked lines](#which-lines-a-tool-edits), each at its own `\pos`. They go into the active line one after another, in Grid order, and the other lines stay as they were.
- **Preview:** holding the pointer over an entry, or moving to it with the keys, previews its result on the video as a filled outline.
- **Choosing an entry** writes the result as one step in the history. If nothing would be left, the video says *Nothing would be left of the drawing* and nothing changes. With neither a clip nor another picked drawing, **Pathfinder** is off.

**Offset**, on the bar, grows the filled drawing by that many script pixels, or shrinks it when negative. **Offset corners** chooses **Miter**, **Round** or **Square** corners, **Round** at first. While **Offset** isn't 0, the result previews on the video. **Apply offset** writes it as one step and sets **Offset** back to 0.

Both fill the drawing as libass does, and write it back as straight lines, with curves turned into short lines. The line's `\pos`, `\an`, scale and `\frz` stay.

## Text on path

Text on path lays the active line's text out along a path: one new line for each letter, standing on the path at its bottom centre and turned with it. A letter keeps its marks, so Thai vowels and tone marks stay on it. Text on path has no key at first; give it one in **File › Settings › Shortcuts**.

The path is the line's vector clip, with all its lines and curves, or one you draw on the video with the point modes of Vector clipping: **Move points**, **Add line**, **Add Bézier curve** and **Delete point**. With neither, the video says *Draw a path, or give the line a vector clip*.

- **Layout:** **Left**, **Centre**, **Right**, or **Spread**, which widens the gaps to fill the path. **Offset** moves the text along it, in pixels.
- **Reverse** runs the path the other way.
- **Rotate**, on at first, turns each letter with the path. Off, the letters keep the line's own `\frz`.
- **Slide** moves the text along the path, from its start to where the text's end meets the path's end, or the other way with **Reverse**. It moves on every **Frames a step** frames, 1 at first, and each step's lines take the times of its frames. While Slide is on, the layout buttons are off. Slide needs an open video: without one, the video says *Slide needs the video's frames*.

Each new line carries the line's earlier override tags, except its position, alignment, origin and clips, and its rotation while **Rotate** is on. Spaces and line breaks only move the next letter on. **Lines** counts the lines that **Apply** will make. Text longer than the path goes straight on past its end, the video says *The text is longer than the path*, and **Past the end** gives how far, in pixels.

The new lines preview over the video, with the original hidden. **Apply**, or <kbd>Enter</kbd> over the video, writes them as one step named **Text on path** in the history: the original line is kept as a comment, and the new lines follow it, selected. <kbd>Esc</kbd> drops the preview and the path, and nothing is written.

## Blur and glow layers

**Subtitles › Blur and glow layers…** builds layers under the selected lines: glows, the border on a layer of its own, and a 3D shadow of stacked copies. It does natively what ua.BlurAndGlow, lyger's BorderSplit and NecrosCopy's 3D shadow do. It is also in the Grid's right-click menu and the Line editor's. The window can stay open while you work. While it is open, the video shows the selected lines with their layers, and **Preview on video** turns that off.

Tick the parts you want:

- **Glow:** blurred copies under each line. Each glow has its **Blur**, a **Border** added to the line's, a **Colour**, which is the line's border colour at first, and an **Alpha** in hex. **Add a glow layer** adds another, up to 8.
- **Border:** the border on its own layer, blurred apart from the fill.
  - **Under the border:** **Border colour**, or **Transparent**, where a thin fill-coloured border closes the gap under the blur.
  - **Border blur** is the line's blur at first. The fill keeps the line's blur.
  - **Hide the fill under the border during fades**, over the number of milliseconds you give it.
  - **Second border** adds a wider border under the first, in its own width and colour. The colour is the line's fill colour at first.
- **Shadow:** a 3D shadow of stacked copies. **Steps** is **Auto** at first, one a pixel of each line's shadow offset, and can be up to 64. The nearest and farthest copies have a colour, the line's shadow colour at first, and an alpha in hex. The copies between them go from one to the other.

**Blur where a line has none** gives the built layers a blur, 0.6 at first, when a line has no `\blur` or `\be`. A line without a border skips Border, and a line whose shadow is under 1 px skips Shadow: the window says how many lines skip them.

**Build layers** builds them all as one undo step, *Blur and glow layers*. The new lines go just above each line in the Grid, on the layers under it. The line itself moves up by the number of lines built under it, and other lines' layers don't change. The clip, the position, the move, the fade, drawings and the text stay on every layer. One build can make up to 20,000 lines.

**Subtitles › Build layers with the last values**, also in the Grid's right-click menu, builds again on the selected lines with the values you last built with, without the window. Neither command has a key at first: give them one in **File › Settings › Shortcuts**.

## Zoom

<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+wheel zooms the video around the pointer, and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+wheel or a middle-button drag pans it. A tool that uses the wheel or the middle button keeps it: Hydra keeps the wheel and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+wheel, Vector clipping and Vector drawing keep the wheel, and Text X / Y rotation and Text scaling keep the middle button. A middle click that doesn't move still reaches the tool, so the Position pointer still puts `\pos` there. **Video › Reset zoom and pan**, or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> over the video, goes back to the whole picture. The tools follow the zoom and the pan.

With **Zoom and pan** set to **As in HikariSub legacy**, the wheel zooms only with the Position pointer on, and **Video › Zoom video** lets you drag and resize a zoom frame; the tools are hidden while you do, and Return ends it. See [Zoom and pan](/en/docs/basics/video#zoom-and-pan).

## Sharp previews

The handles and guides are drawn over the picture at the screen's resolution, so they stay sharp at any zoom, and keep their size on a high-DPI screen.

The subtitles themselves are drawn at the video's resolution and scaled with the picture, as in most players. On a large or high-DPI screen they can look soft. To draw them at the size the picture takes on screen instead, set **File › Settings › Video › Subtitles on video › Preview subtitles at** to **Screen size (sharp)**. The layout is the same either way, and so are copied or saved frames, which stay at the video's size. The setting also applies to full screen and to the previews in the Style manager, the font picker and the Template editor. It is available with libass, not with a VSFilter renderer.

## Undo and cancelling

Each drag is one step in the history, named after the tool, such as *Visual scaling tool*. So is a typed or scrubbed value. If the Line editor has changes you have not applied, they are applied first, as a step of their own.

Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> during a drag to drop it; the handles go back and nothing is written.
